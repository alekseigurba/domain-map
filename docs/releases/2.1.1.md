# 2.1.1

Icons on the map grow up. An icon used to be a small black mark over a title,
paid for with one of the title's rows; it is now drawn at a size that holds its
own against a word, in the colours it was drawn in, on whichever side of the
title suits the shape, and it takes its room from the parts of the shape the
words could never use.

Underneath is one change of model: an icon and its title are laid out as a
pair, inside the shape they are actually in, and by what is actually drawn —
the ink of the icon and the letters of the title — rather than as two boxes
stacked in a third.

Nothing breaks, and there is nothing to run.

## Breaking changes

None. Map files stay version `2`. The two new fields are ones a file may leave
out, and a 2.1.0 deployment reads past them: it opens a map written here and
draws every icon over its title, as its file has it.

## Upgrading

Nothing to run: no environment variable, no migration, no change to a stored
version until an owner saves it.

What changes is how a map already drawn looks on the day of the upgrade:

- Every icon is larger, and in colour. An icon chosen because its silhouette
  read well in black may want another look against the fill it sits on.
- A title under an icon gets back the row the icon took. One that was cut with
  an ellipsis may now read in full, and one whose type was set down a size to
  make it fit can have it set back up.
- An actor's figure is nearly a third larger.

Rolling back to 2.1.0 loses nothing but the look: the placement and the weight
stay in the file, unread, and come back with the upgrade.

## How it works

### The pair

- The words come first. The title is wrapped into the room left by the
  *smallest* icon, and the icon then takes whatever the words did not, up to its
  full size — half as large again as the type. Where a shape really is full the
  icon gives way, down to the size of the type and no further.
- Adding an icon used to cost a title a row outright. That sent people to a
  smaller font to get the row back, and the icon, sized off the font, shrank
  with it — which is how a map ended up with type nobody could read under icons
  nobody could see.
- The pair is laid out in the shape, not in the box inside it. The widest
  rectangle that fits an oval is what the words wrap into, and always was; but
  an icon is small, so it goes up into the crown of the oval, or out into the
  end of it, where a row of words would never fit. That room was always there
  and nothing used it.
- A short row at the far end may sit further out than a full one — the "Engine"
  of "Checkout Engine" lower than a second row the width of the first — and
  what it gives up goes to the icon.
- The pair straddles the middle of the shape for as long as it fits there. One
  too long for that moves towards the icon's side, where the room is; one too
  long for the shape altogether is centred in what room there is, so it spills
  evenly, as text too big for its oval always has.
- A touchpoint is a box and has no crown: whatever the icon's size, the way to
  the edge is the same, so the pair gets the room the words get and no more.
- Over a sample of titles, type sizes and shapes, an icon used to change what
  the title said in a little over a quarter of them. It now does in fewer than
  one in ten, all of them shapes that were already full.

### By its ink

- An icon is laid out by what is drawn in it, not by its file. Most icons are
  drawn with a margin round them, and a wide drawing in a square file has a
  great deal of nothing over and under it. Laid out by the file, that margin
  turned up on the map as a gap between icon and title that nobody had chosen.
- The gap is now the one the map chose, and a small one: a fifth of the type
  size over or under a title, a little more beside one. It is measured from ink
  to letters. A row of type is a box taller than its capitals, so the leading
  that box carries on the icon's side is taken off rather than added on.
- A drawing far from square is given its long side. A wide one gets a wide,
  short box of the same area a square one would have, up to half as long again
  as the square; held to a square it came out a sliver.
- Finding the ink takes a canvas: the icon is drawn small and looked over for
  what is not transparent. That is the diagram's business, in
  `app/js/icon-art.js`; `geometry.js` only keeps what was found, the way it
  keeps the palette, and stays pure. Until an icon has been measured it is taken
  for its whole file, square — a worse layout for one frame, not a missing icon.
- A picture with no transparency — a photograph — is all ink, which is the
  right answer for it.

### Placement

- **Icon placement** is over the title, left of it, under it or right of it. It
  is in the Shape section: of a capability and a touchpoint once they wear an
  icon, since until then there is nothing to place, and of an actor always.
- Beside the title, the icon goes out into the end of the oval and the title
  gives up a little width and none of its rows. Every row stays centred on one
  line, and the middle rows of an oval may run further out than the first and
  last, as the shape allows.
- Under is over seen in a mirror, and right is left: one layout, turned round.
  So the four behave alike, and a fix to one is a fix to all of them.
- Over the title is where an icon has always been. A map that never chooses is
  drawn exactly as it would have been, and writes the file it always wrote.

### Colours

- An icon keeps the colours it was drawn in, on the map and in Export SVG. It
  used to be flattened to the title's black, which turned a full-colour drawing
  into a silhouette, and a set of icons told apart by colour into one icon.
- The title stays black on every fill, as before: a label that flips to white on
  the darker swatches reads as a different kind of thing. The icon is the part
  of the shape that is allowed a colour of its own.

### Icon weight

- A slider under the icon picker, from 0.5× to 4× in quarters: how heavy an SVG
  icon's lines are drawn, as a multiple of what its file says.
- A multiple rather than a width, because a width means nothing without the
  grid it is on: 2 is a bold line on a 24-unit icon and a fine one on a 64.
- An icon on the map is an `<image>`, a document of its own that no stylesheet
  here can reach. So a heavier line is not a style: it is another drawing, made
  from the file with every stroke width multiplied, and pointed at in place of
  the file. The file is parsed and never put in the page, so nothing in it runs.
- A heavier line reaches further out, and a drawing that ran close to the edge
  of its file would be clipped by it. The drawing's own frame grows by what the
  lines gained; since an icon is laid out by its ink, the wider margin costs
  nothing.
- Each weight is measured as a drawing of its own, since a heavier one covers a
  little more of its picture.
- A picture that is not an SVG has no lines to weigh, and is not offered the
  slider.
- It sits under the picker rather than among the shape's controls because it is
  there to make up for a file, and belongs beside the file. It is a stopgap for
  icons drawn with a line too fine for the map, and the better fix is the file.

### Actors

- An actor's figure is laid out the way an icon is: it gives way to a long name,
  goes out past the box the name wraps into, and can be placed on any of the
  four sides. It has no file, so it has no weight.
- The silhouette is drawn on a 24-unit grid and fills only the middle of it. It
  is drawn cropped to its ink now, so the size the layout hands over is the size
  of what is seen, and the air round the figure is air the layout chose.

### Drawing an icon for the map

- A line of 1.5 to 2 on a 24-unit grid, round caps, in colours that hold against
  the palette. At 0.75 a line is a hairline once the map is zoomed out, and in
  colour it has no black to fall back on.
- The size of the grid does not matter — it is a unit, not a size — and neither,
  now, does the margin: the map lays an icon out by its ink.
- Button icons are another convention: a 16-unit grid, and no stroke or colour
  of their own, since the page supplies both. A file drawn for one is the wrong
  weight as the other.

### The file

- `version` stays `2`.
- `shape.iconPlacement`: `top`, `left`, `bottom` or `right`, on a capability, a
  touchpoint and an actor. Left out when it is `top`.
- `shape.iconWeight`: 0.5 to 4, on a capability and a touchpoint. Left out when
  it is 1.
- Anything else in either field is refused, with the element named, before
  anything in the file is applied.
- The example map was redrawn, and the demo in the README recorded again over
  it. The seed fills an empty store once, so a map already in the database opens
  as it was saved.

### Export

- **Export SVG** carries each icon inside the file in its own colours and at the
  weight it is drawn. The filter that flattened an icon to black in the exported
  file is gone along with the one on screen.
- **Export** (JSON) names an icon, where it sits and how heavy it is drawn. It
  does not carry the file, as before.

## Left for later

- Icon weight is a stopgap, and sits where a stopgap sits. If it stays, it wants
  a place among the shape's controls and a say in *Reset shapes*.
- *Reset shapes* leaves placement and weight alone, as it leaves the icon alone.
- An icon's size follows the type and cannot be set on its own.
- A heavier drawing is made once per weight and kept for as long as the tab is
  open. Dragging the slider through every stop on one icon keeps every stop.
