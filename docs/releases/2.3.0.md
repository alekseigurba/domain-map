# 2.3.0

An outline, to be put into the owner's words. The map gains a third layer,
**Product Areas**: who owns what. An area is a band drawn round the domains,
touchpoints and loose capabilities that one team holds, so the map that says
what the business does can also say how the organisation that builds it is cut.
Every decision below was settled with the owner on 2026-09-21; none of it is
built. Building starts once 2.2.0 is committed, so that this is a diff of its
own.

## Breaking changes

- The document version goes from `2` to `3`, and every save writes `3`. A 2.1 or 2.2 deployment refuses a layer key it does not know, and would read past an `areas` list and write the map back without it — a whole organisation lost on the next save, silently. Refusing the file with "this app reads up to 2" is the honest way round.
- The break is one-way, as 2.1.0's was: versions 1 and 2 still read, as a map with no areas and the new layer shown.

## The model

- A third layer under the other two: **Product Areas**, key `areas`, holding one kind of element, the `area`. Layer order is paint order, so an area is the ground the domains stand on, and a fill tints the paper rather than the shapes on it.
- Business Domains stays the base layer — never hidden, where Edit mode opens — but by name now rather than by being first in the list. Product Areas can be hidden and dimmed like the Presentation Layer.
- Membership is explicit. A domain, a touchpoint or a loose capability names its area; an area has no size of its own and is drawn round what it holds, as a domain is drawn round its capabilities.
- One area at most, and areas are flat: they never nest and never share a member, so the map answers "who owns this" with one name or with nobody.
- A capability inside a domain belongs through its domain — a domain is never split between two areas. An actor stands outside the business, so no area holds one.
- Nothing connects to an area.
- An area carries what a domain does: title, description, owner, and a Type from a list of its own.

## The band

- The outline is a band stretched round its members: the tightest rounded line that holds every one of them, padded past each member's real outline — a domain's blob, not its middle — and straight between them. It never spikes, however far apart the members sit. It reads as a boundary, not as terrain, which is what tells it from a domain.
- A solid border in the area's swatch, and a wash of the same colour inside it. Solid because dashes are already chrome here: an empty slot, the lobe handles, a line being drawn. The wash is what still reads when a large map is zoomed out and the line has gone thin.
- Opacity runs 0 to 100 for an area, where every other shape stops at 10: 0 is no fill at all. A new area opens at 10.
- The title rides the rim like a legend: one line, astride the border, with the line broken behind it. It takes no room inside and cannot be mistaken for a domain's title.
- The title wears the border's colour, deepened until it holds 4.5:1 against the paper. A blue line keeps a blue title; a yellow one gets a dark olive. Half the palette is too pale to read as type on its own.
- Shape attributes: colour, opacity, font size and weight on the domain's scale, and Title size. A place round the rim stands in for a domain's title position. There is no title width — one line does not wrap — and no kebab, since there are no lobes to edit.
- An area with nothing in it is a small band at a position of its own. When its last member leaves it stays where it was drawn rather than jumping back to where it was first added.
- The first area wears the default colour; each one after it takes a swatch no other area does, since two neighbouring teams in one colour are one team to the eye.

## Handling it

- **Area**, among the Add buttons, when the Product Areas layer is the one picked.
- Only the line and the title are handles. The inside of an area is open ground: it pans the map and belongs to the shapes standing on it.
- Dragging the border carries everything the area holds — every member moves by the same amount, as one undo step. It is the only thing moving an area can mean when its outline is drawn round its members.
- Dragging the title slides it round the rim. Double-click renames it where it sits; Enter commits.
- A shape joins an area by being brought in: a drag that starts outside the line and ends inside it, with the rim lighting up on the area about to receive it. Dropped inside another area's line it changes hands. Dropped on open ground nothing changes — the band stretches after it. Nudging a shape that already sits inside a line it does not belong to never joins it.
- Leaving is always said in the details, never by dragging. Tidying the map must not reorganise the company.
- A capability dropped into a domain gives up an area of its own and belongs through the domain. One pulled out of a domain keeps the domain's area until told otherwise: leaving a domain is not leaving the team.
- Deleting an area frees what it held and deletes none of it. Undo puts the memberships back.

## Details and menu

- The details of a domain, a touchpoint and a loose capability gain an **Area** field: one of the map's areas, or none. For a capability inside a domain it is read-only and names the area it belongs to through its domain.
- An area's details are a domain's — label, title, description, owner, Type, layer — with what it holds listed as chips that select the shape, and a count of each kind.
- The menu gains a flat **Product Areas** section after Actors, one row per area. Every shape keeps exactly one row in the menu; what an area holds is in its details.
- The footer's count of shapes names areas too.

## The Assistant

- The brief lists the areas, and says which one each domain, touchpoint and loose capability is in, so a review of the boundaries can see the teams as well.
- `describe`, `rename` and `set-type` work on an area, named to the model as `area:payments`. An area can be the subject of *Describe*, *Fill the blanks* and *Tighten the wording*.
- No `add-area`, and nothing that hands a shape to an area. Who owns what is the owner's meaning, as position is.

## The file

- `areas`, written before `domains` and only when the map has any, each with a key, its text, and a `shape` of position, `titleAngle`, colour, size, weight, Title size and opacity. The angle is what keeps the title on its side of the band as the band changes shape.
- `area: <key>` on a domain, a touchpoint and a loose capability. On a capability that has a domain it is not written, and not read.
- `layers` lists three, bottom first: `areas`, `core`, `presentation`. `types` may carry a list for `area`.
- The validator refuses an `area` that names no area in the file, as it does a `domain` that names no domain.

## The example map

## Where it lands

- `rules.js`: the kind, the layer, `BASE_LAYER` by name, `AREA_DEFAULTS`, `validateArea` with its own opacity floor. `defaults.js`: `AREA_SHAPE`, which makes it part of what a brand may restyle — a row in BRANDING.md and a block in `tests/defaults.test.mjs`.
- `store.js`: the list, create, update and delete, membership, the move that carries its members, `restore` putting memberships back. `document.js`: reading, writing, validating, version 3.
- `geometry.js`, pure and under test: the band round a set of outlines, the gap in it behind the title, the point on the rim at an angle, whether a point is inside, and the deepened ink.
- `diagram.js`: a stack of its own at the bottom of the new layer, the border and title as hit targets, the drags, the receiving rim, bounds for *Fit to screen*. `svg-export.js`: the new hit targets join the chrome that is stripped.
- `main.js`, `details.js`, `menu.js`: the tables that name each kind, the Add button and its icon, the Area field, the chips, the new section. `suggestions.js` and `app/skills/`: the brief, the subjects, the two format pages.
- `tests/layers.test.mjs` asserts two layers and version 2 in a dozen places; all of them move.
- `design-system.md`: the band, its border-width token, the legend, the 0–100 opacity; the Shape defaults chapter, which already trails the code for touchpoints and actors. `README.md`, the in-app help, which says "the two layers", and the changelog.

## What it will not do

- Nest areas, share a member between two, or split a domain between teams.
- Keep a stranger out. A band is convex, so a shape parked between two members sits inside the line and under the wash without belonging. The Area field tells the truth, and the cure is to move the stranger. Worth a known issue on this page when it ships.
- Let a model draw the organisation: no `add-area`, no assigning, no skill that grills ownership against the domain boundaries. That is a release of its own in size.
