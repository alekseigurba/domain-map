# 2.3.0

The map gains a third layer, **Product Areas**: who owns what. An area is a band
drawn round the domains, touchpoints and loose capabilities one team holds, so
the map that says what the business does can also say how the organisation that
builds it is cut — and the two can be held against each other.

It is the third layer 2.1 said would be a release rather than a row in a file,
and it answers the two questions that page left for it. Nothing connects to an
area: it is not a step a flow passes through, it is whose ground the flow is
crossing. And for every map already drawn it means nothing at all until someone
adds one — the layer is there, shown and empty.

The menu and the details panel are also a fifth wider, and Ctrl+Z after a drag
works.

## Breaking changes

| What | Was | Is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Document version | `2` | `3` |
| Reading a map file | any 2.1 or 2.2 deployment | 2.3 and later. A 2.2 deployment refuses a version 3 file. |
| Layers | `core`, `presentation` | `areas` under them both |
| Elements | `domains`, `capabilities`, `touchpoints`, `actors` | `areas` alongside them, and an `area` on the shapes one holds |

The version moved because the alternative was worse. A 2.2 deployment refuses a
layer key it does not know, with a sentence about layers that would puzzle
anyone who met it; and had the key been left out, it would have read past the
`areas` list and written the map back without it — a whole organisation lost on
the next save, silently. "This file is version 3; this app reads up to 2" is the
honest way round. 2.2's description of the map went the other way, and was
right to: a field read past and dropped is a sentence lost, not a structure.

The break is one-way, as 2.1's was. 2.3 reads every version 1 and version 2 file
there is, as a map with no areas. Every save writes version 3, whether or not
the map draws any: a version that depended on the content would be a rule to
explain, for the sake of a file an older app could open only until somebody
added a team. Keep a 2.2-era export of anything that has to travel backwards.

## Upgrading

Nothing to run: no new environment variable, no migration, no new dependency.
Stored versions stay as they were written until an owner saves them, as in 2.1.

A brand that shadows `js/defaults.js` has no `AREA_SHAPE` in it. The app reads
it off the module rather than asking for it by name, so the file still loads and
a new area falls back on the package's own look. Add the block when you want
another; `tests/defaults.test.mjs` checks it like the rest.

## How it works

### The model

- Three layers, bottom first: **Product Areas**, **Business Domains**, the
  **Presentation Layer**. Layer order is paint order, so an area is the ground
  the domains stand on. Painted on top instead, its wash would tint every shape
  and title under it; underneath, it tints the paper.
- Business Domains is still the base layer — the one that cannot be hidden, and
  where Edit mode opens — but by name now, where it used to be so by being first
  in the list. Who owns the business can be put away; what it does cannot.
- Membership is explicit. A domain, a touchpoint or a loose capability names the
  area it is in, in the file too. An area has no size of its own and is drawn
  round whatever names it, the way a domain is drawn round its capabilities.
- One area at most, and areas are flat: they never nest and never share a
  member, so the map answers "who owns this" with one name or with nobody.
- A capability inside a domain belongs through its domain — a domain is never
  split between two areas. An actor stands outside the business, so no area
  holds one.
- An area carries what a domain does: title, description, owner, and a Type from
  a list of its own.

### The band

- The outline is a **band** stretched round its members: the tightest rounded
  line that holds every one of them, stood off from each member's real outline —
  a domain's blob, not its middle — and straight between them. A domain's blob
  is sampled along rays from its middle, which reaches a far-off lobe with a
  spike; members of an area sit much further apart than capabilities in a domain
  do, and a band never spikes however far that is. It reads as a boundary rather
  than as terrain, which is what tells it from a domain.
- It stands 64px off what it holds: room enough to read as a line round a team
  rather than as an outline of its shapes, which at half that it did. It is most
  of the gap the app itself leaves between two domains, so on a map packed that
  tightly the bands of two teams side by side overlap — see the known issues.
- The band goes round what can be seen. A touchpoint on a hidden Presentation
  Layer is still a member, but the line does not reach out to a shape nobody can
  see.
- A solid border in the area's swatch, and a wash of the same colour inside it.
  Solid, because dashes are already chrome here: an empty slot, the lobe
  handles, a line being drawn. The wash is what still reads when a large map is
  zoomed out and the line has gone thin, and it tints each member towards its
  team.
- Opacity runs 0 to 100 for an area, where every other shape stops at 10: 0 is a
  border and no wash at all. A new area opens at 10.
- The title rides the border like a legend: one line, astride the line, with the
  border broken behind it. It takes no room inside and cannot be mistaken for a
  domain's title. Where it sits is kept as an angle seen from the middle of the
  band, so it keeps its side as the band changes shape under it.
- The title wears the border's colour, **deepened** until it holds 4.5:1 against
  the paper. Half the palette is too pale to read as type: a blue line keeps a
  blue title, a yellow one gets a dark olive, and either still belongs to its
  line.
- Of a domain's shape attributes an area keeps colour, opacity, font size and
  weight, and Title size — which on an area sizes the words themselves, there
  being no lobe round a title that rides a border. There is no title width, since
  one line does not wrap, and no ⋮ menu, since there are no lobes to arrange.
- An area with nothing in it is a small band at a position of its own, just long
  enough for its title. Emptied out, it takes the place of the last shape to
  leave it rather than jumping back to wherever it was first added.
- The first area wears the default colour; each one after it takes the next
  swatch no other area does. Two teams side by side in one colour are one team
  to the eye.

### Handling it

- **Add an area**, among the Add buttons, when Product Areas is the layer
  picked.
- Only the border and the title are handles. The inside of an area is open
  ground: it pans the map, and belongs to the shapes standing on it.
- Dragging the border carries everything the area holds — every member by the
  same amount, as one undo step. It is the only thing moving an area can mean
  when its outline is drawn round its members.
- Dragging the title slides it round the border. Double-click renames it where
  it sits; any Enter commits, since there is one line to put it on.
- A shape joins by being **brought in**: a drag that starts outside the line and
  ends inside it, with the rim lighting up on the area about to receive it.
  Dropped inside another area's line it changes hands. Dropped on open ground
  nothing changes, and the band stretches after it. A shape that already sits
  inside a line it does not belong to is not taken for being nudged.
- **Leaving is said in the details, never by dragging.** A capability leaves its
  domain by being dropped outside it; done the same way here, dragging a domain
  far from its neighbours — ordinary tidying — would take it out of its team.
- A capability dropped into a domain gives up an area of its own and belongs
  through the domain. One pulled out of a domain keeps the domain's area until
  told otherwise: leaving a domain is not leaving the team. A domain about to
  take a capability in beats an area it is over.
- Deleting an area frees what it held and deletes none of it, so it does not
  ask. Undo puts the area back and every membership with it.

### Details and menu

- The details of a domain, a touchpoint and a loose capability gain an **Area**
  field: one of the map's areas, or none. For a capability inside a domain it is
  read-only, and names the area it has through its domain.
- An area's details are a domain's — label, title, description, owner, Type,
  layer — with a **Holds** section between what it is and how it looks: what it
  holds, as names to press, each showing its shape on the map. They stay live
  while browsing; the foot of the panel counts each kind.
- The menu gains a flat **Product Areas** section after Actors, one row per
  area, its swatch a border in the area's colour. Every shape keeps exactly one
  row in the menu; what an area holds is in its details, not listed twice.
- The footer's count names areas once there are any.

### The Assistant

- The brief lists the areas, and says which one each domain, touchpoint and
  loose capability is in, so a review of the boundaries can see the teams as
  well. A map that draws no organisation sends no list rather than an empty one.
- `describe`, `rename` and `set-type` work on an area, named to the model as
  `area:money-movement`. An area can be the subject of *Describe*, *Fill the
  blanks* and *Tighten the wording*.
- There is no `add-area`, and nothing that hands a shape to an area. Who owns
  what is the owner's meaning, as position is — and the brief tells the model
  so, and to put what it thinks in a note.

### The file

```json
{
  "version": 3,
  "layers": [{ "key": "areas" }, { "key": "core" }, { "key": "presentation", "hidden": true }],
  "areas": [
    {
      "key": "money-movement",
      "title": "Money Movement",
      "shape": { "position": "0,0", "titleAngle": 90, "color": 7, "size": 48, "weight": "bold", "titleScale": 1, "opacity": 10 }
    }
  ],
  "domains": [{ "key": "core-ledger-repayment", "area": "money-movement", "title": "Core Ledger\n& Repayment" }]
}
```

- `areas` is written before `domains`, and only when the map has any.
  `position` is where the area sits while it holds nothing; round its members
  it is not looked at. `titleAngle` is degrees clockwise from the right, to a
  tenth, and is written only once the title has been slid — left out, it rides
  the top.
- `area` on a domain, a touchpoint and a loose capability. On a capability that
  has a domain it is not written, and not read. An `area` that names nothing in
  the file is refused, as a `domain` that names nothing is.
- `types` may carry a list for `area`.

### The example map

The seed draws three areas — **Checkout & Risk**, **Money Movement** and
**Customer & Merchant** — holding between them a domain, a touchpoint and a loose
capability, so every kind of member is on show. The map was opened out to give
them room: drawn as tightly as it was, the bands ran into each other. It opens
with the Presentation Layer shown and Product Areas dimmed, so the teams sit
behind the business rather than over it. The Customer App and the Back office
belong to nobody: they stand in a column at the left, and a band reaching them
from any team would run across another team's domain. Only a fresh store is
seeded from it.

### Wider panels

The menu and the details panel are `--panel-w`: **300px**, where they were 250,
and never more than 24% of the window, so a small laptop keeps a stage between
them. A capability's title and a field's label are read rather than cut short.

### Undo after a drag

Ctrl+Z after dragging a domain, a touchpoint or an actor said *Undid: moving*
and left the shape where it had been dropped. A drag moves the record as it
goes, so that the map follows the pointer; the undo step was then written from
the record, which by then held the new position. The diagram hands over where
the drag set off from now, and that is what undo puts back. A capability was
never affected — its drop wrote down where it had been before anything moved.

### Under the hood

- `geometry.js` gains the band and what goes with it, pure and under test: the
  hull of a set of points grown by a disc, the border with its title's room
  taken out of it, the point of the border at an angle, whether a point is
  inside, and the deepened ink. `layoutDomain` hands over the points its blob
  was sampled at, which is what a band is drawn round.
- `rules.js` has the kind, the layer and `validateArea`; `BASE_LAYER` is `'core'`
  by name. `store.js` has the list, membership, and the two rules about a
  capability changing home. Undo records are plain data and cannot clear a field
  with a null, so leaving an area takes a `clearArea` flag, as leaving a domain
  takes `clearDomain`.
- Each layer renders one more stack, `stack--areas`, first. Hover and selection
  are a halo under the border, so the area keeps its own colour inside it. The
  hit targets join the chrome that Export SVG strips.
- The server, the version store and the undo history know nothing about kinds,
  and did not change.

## What it does not do

- Nest areas, share a member between two, or split a domain between teams.
- Let a model draw the organisation. There is no `add-area`, no assigning, and
  no skill that grills ownership against the domain boundaries — a model can be
  asked through *Ask your own*, and answers in notes.
- Hold its line weight. An area's border is a fixed number of map units, like a
  connector's, and thins as the map is zoomed out. The wash is what carries it
  then; the fix belongs with the connectors', in the backlog.

## Known issues

- **A band is convex, so it can take in what it does not hold.** A shape parked
  between two members of a team sits inside their line and under their wash
  without belonging. And a band stands 64px off what it holds, which is most of
  the room the app leaves between two domains — so on a tightly packed map the
  bands of neighbouring teams overlap, and a line passes over the edge of a
  shape it does not hold. The example map did both until it was opened out. The
  Area field tells the truth, and the cure is room: move the shape, or spread
  the map.
- A title longer than the stretch of border it rides hangs over the corners
  either side. *Title size* or a shorter name brings it back.
