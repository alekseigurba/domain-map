# 2.5.1

The list of people gets the two things 2.5.0 left it without: more than one
person to run it, and a way to take someone off it. 2.5.0 had one
administrator, held there by the database, so the list depended on one person
being around to change a role; now there can be any number, and the map is
never left with none. And where 2.5.0's floor was viewer — a person who had
left sat on the list with when they were last here — an administrator can now
remove them, and their sandbox goes with them.

Around it, Users & access says what each role may do, and the Versions dialog
is wide enough again for everything a publisher can do with a shared version.

## Breaking changes

| What | Was | Is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Administrators | one, held by a unique index | any number, and never none |
| Handing over | *Make administrator*, which made you a publisher | make someone an administrator; they take you down, or not |
| `PUT /api/administrator` | hands the one administrator's role over | gone. `PATCH /api/people/<id>` with `{ "role": "administrator" }` makes another |
| `DELETE /api/people/<id>` | not there | an administrator takes someone off the list |
| `npm run make-administrator` | made someone the administrator, and the one before a publisher | makes someone an administrator, and changes nobody else |

Map files stay version `4`, and a 2.5.0 deployment opens one written here.

## Upgrading

One migration, `004-administrators.sql`, applied at startup like the others:
it drops the index that allowed one administrator. Nothing else to run, and
nobody's role changes on the day — the administrator there was is the one
there is, until they make another.

Rolling back to 2.5.0 leaves the index dropped, and 2.5.0 does not expect two
administrators: its *Make administrator* demotes one of them and leaves the
rest. Bring them down to one before going back.

## How it works

### More than one administrator

- The role select on a row offers **Administrator** as it offers the other
  three, and so does the form that adds someone by email. Making someone an
  administrator asks first: they can then do everything you can, taking you
  off the list included, and a select changes on an arrow key. Any other
  change of role is made as it is picked, and undone from the same select.
- **Nobody changes or removes their own row.** Their row reads as it is, as
  the one administrator's did, and another administrator does it. That is what
  keeps the map from being left with nobody to run it: whoever changes a role
  is still an administrator afterwards. Stepping down is asking another
  administrator.
- Handing over was one button in 2.5.0 and is two steps now: make the new
  administrator one, and they take the old one down — or both stay. *Make
  administrator* is gone, and with it `PUT /api/administrator`.
- The database no longer holds the line; the people store does. Every change
  to a role or to the list takes one lock for the length of its transaction,
  so two administrators taking each other down in the same moment leave one of
  them rather than none — the second sees the first's change and is refused.
  And a change that would take away the last administrator is refused, naming
  them. From the app that can only happen with sign-in off, where whoever is
  there is an administrator by the configuration rather than by a row.
- The development bypass gives the role asked for, administrator included, and
  no longer hands it over: asking for it makes one more. The last
  administrator asking for less stays one, as the first person in always did.
- `npm run make-administrator -- someone@example.com` makes someone an
  administrator and leaves everyone else as they are, and says whether they
  were one already. It is still for the day there is nobody left in the app
  who can.

### Taking someone off the list

- **Remove**, a chip on every row but your own, for an administrator.
- Someone added by email who never signed in goes at once. They are a line on
  a list and nothing more: no sandbox, no login, nothing they shared.
- Someone who has signed in is asked about first, and the question says what
  their sandbox holds. The versions in it are deleted with them — a sandbox is
  nobody else's to see, so there would be nobody left who could open them — and
  the question says how many, or that it is empty.
- What they shared stays shared, with nobody as its sharer. Renaming or
  deleting it is a publisher's from then on, as it is for a version shared
  before sandboxes existed — and theirs again should they come back under the
  same address, since a version with no sharer goes by who saved it. The
  published version is untouched whoever shared it.
- A domain or capability they owned keeps the owner's name. The note under the
  Owner box says they are not on the list any more, as it does for any id the
  list does not have, and the names behind the box drop them — in this tab at
  once, and in any other at its next load, as with every change to the list.
- **Removing is not locking out.** The list is not the gate — being assigned to
  the application in Entra is, as it has been since 2.5.0 made everyone who
  signs in a contributor. Someone who can still sign in, or who still has the
  map open, comes back at their next click as anyone new does: a contributor,
  with an empty sandbox. The question says so. To keep someone out, take their
  assignment away in Entra; to keep them in and read-only, make them a viewer
  and leave them on the list.

### What each role may do

- A key at the head of Users & access: the four roles, lowest first, each with
  a sentence of what it may do. Each one up the ladder says what it adds —
  *does what a contributor does, and chooses which shared version everyone
  sees* — since it holds everything below it.
- It is there for everyone who can open the dialog, not only the
  administrator: a contributor reading the list is as likely to be wondering
  whom to ask.
- The same sentences are on the pointer over a role on a row, and in the
  profile popup under your name, where the line used to be a few words. One
  set of words in `identity.js`, so the three never say different things.

### The Versions dialog

- 920px wide, where it was 760. 2.5.0 gave each list a column saying who saved
  or shared a version, and on a shared row a publisher has five actions: at
  760px, Publish and Delete were behind a horizontal scroll bar. A shared row
  with all five and all three tags is what the width is measured against.
- The name column is 16rem, where it was 12. A version tagged Published, Open
  and Yours showed one letter of its name; it now shows a word or two, with the
  whole of it on the pointer as before.
- Users & access stays at 760px. A row there carries one action, not five, and
  the table fits.
- On a phone both lists are cards, as they were meant to be: a long version
  name is cut where the card ends rather than carrying the card off the edge,
  a shared version's actions wrap onto a second line, and on the roster the
  row's button sits under the facts instead of being drawn over the date.

## Known issues

- Removing someone cannot be undone. The versions in their sandbox are gone,
  and a person put back by email is someone new: what they owned on the map
  does not find them again until they are picked in the Owner box anew.
- The two lists in Versions are separate tables, so their columns line up only
  as far as their contents happen to: a sandbox row has four actions and a
  shared row five, and the dates sit a little apart.
