# 2.3.1

The layers are **Presentation**, **Domains** and **Areas**, and an area's title
can be broken into rows. Product Areas was named for the product organisation
it was first drawn for; what it holds is any way an organisation is cut — a
team, a tribe, a product line, a business unit — and the plain word says so.
The other two lost their qualifiers to match: three layers, three words. The
title is the smaller change to say and the larger one to draw: a legend that
rides a border has to decide which side of the line its second row goes.

Nothing breaks, and there is nothing to run.

## Breaking changes

None. Map files stay version `3`. A map file never carried a layer's name —
only its key, and `areas`, `core` and `presentation` are as they were — and a
title is a string that could always hold a line break, as a domain's does. A
2.3.0 deployment opens a map written here and draws a broken title on one row,
as it drew every title.

## Upgrading

Nothing to run: no environment variable, no migration, no change to a stored
version until an owner saves it. The layer control, the menu and the details
panel say Presentation, Domains and Areas the moment the page loads.

## How it works

### The names

- **Areas** in the layer control and as the menu section, and **Area** as the
  heading of an area's details, beside Domain. Everywhere else the word was
  already *area* — Add an area, the Area field on a shape, New area — and
  stays.
- **Presentation** and **Domains** for the other two, where they were the
  Presentation Layer and Business Domains. A qualifier that every row carries
  says nothing, and the control reads as a list of three things rather than
  three phrases.
- The Assistant's brief and skills say *area* too. What the model is handed has
  the same shape: the `areas` list, and `area:money-movement` for a reference.
- The 2.3.0 notes and changelog say Product Areas and Business Domains, as
  they did when they shipped.

### The legend

- A title breaks where Shift+Enter or Ctrl+Enter is pressed while renaming it
  on the map, as a domain's does; Enter saves. In the details panel the title
  reads as one line, as a domain's does, and what is typed there keeps the rows
  after the same words.
- Nothing wraps on its own. There are no width bars and no `titleWidth` on an
  area: a legend row is as long as its words, and the break in the border
  follows the longest row. A two-row title is the answer to a team name too
  long for the stretch of border it rides, which 2.3.0 could meet only with a
  smaller size or a shorter name.
- The rows stand **outside** the band, and the nearest of them rides the line
  where the one row always did. At the top of the band the block stands above
  the line; at the bottom it hangs below it. Rows stack upright, so at either
  side there is no outside for them to stand in, and the block sits astride the
  line.
- Between those positions the block shades from one to the other with the
  angle the title is slid to, rather than jumping a row as it crosses the
  middle. The offset is the sine of the title's angle times half the height of
  the rows beyond the first: nothing at the sides, the whole of it at the top
  and the bottom. One row has no rows beyond the first, and draws exactly where
  it did.
- The border is broken behind the whole block, as wide as the longest row, and
  the title's handle and the in-place editor cover the block.
- Outside rather than inside because the inside is the team's ground, with the
  members on it: a second row over a member's shape would read as part of it.
  Astride rather than outside at the sides because the alternative was a block
  standing off the line, joined to it by nothing.

### The file

- `version` stays `3`.
- An area's `title` may hold `\n`, as a domain's may. It is written as it was
  typed.

### The example map

- Customer & Merchant rides the top of its band as two rows, so a fresh store
  shows a stacked legend. Only a fresh store is seeded from it.

## Known issues

- A single row longer than the stretch of border it rides still hangs over the
  corners either side, as [2.3.0](2.3.0.md) says. A break is now the third
  remedy, beside *Title size* and a shorter name.
