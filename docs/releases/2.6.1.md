# 2.6.1

2.6.0 put three new things on the map — features, lines that end anywhere, a
level for how much of them is drawn — and this is what using them showed. The
lines a coarser level draws are put right: they cross the gap between the two
shapes they join, they never end in the middle of a domain, and they are told
apart from the lines somebody drew. A feature leans as a capability does. A
domain keeps a smooth outline when its title is dragged off it. And how a line
is drawn is set beside the line, not a panel away from it.

Two things are for whoever runs the map rather than whoever draws it. The
**Assistant's model** is switched on and off, and given a new key, from inside
the app — the key had been a variable of the deployment, and a key expires
sooner than a release is cut. And **Users & access** is a list that can be
searched, narrowed, read a page at a time, and cleared of the people who have
not been back.

The Assistant itself gains two skills, and every skill is told what an area is
for and how a line is worded.

## Breaking changes

None to a map or to a deployment. What a repo built on the package will see
change:

| What | Was | Is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A line the level drew | solid, like any line | broken, so it is told from one somebody drew |
| Where such a line ends | each end facing the other shape's middle | the pair of connection points that lie closest |
| A line's Shape section | shut, with **Line** a list | open, with **Line** a toggle |
| Users & access | 760px, the role key at its head | 1000px, the role key at its foot |
| `ASSISTANT_API_URL` with no `ASSISTANT_API_KEY` | no model, and nowhere to give it a key | no model until an administrator gives it one in the app |
| `AUTH_SESSION_SECRET` | signs a session | also seals a key kept in the app |
| `/api/assistant/settings`, `/api/assistant/settings/check` | not there | an administrator's |
| `/api/people/inactive` | not there | an administrator's |

Map files stay version `5`. A feature's lean is a field the file may leave
out, written only for a feature that is not wide, so a map that never leaned a
feature writes the file it always wrote, and a 2.6.0 deployment opens one that
did: it reads past the field and draws the feature wide.

## Upgrading

One migration, `005-assistant.sql`, applied at startup like the others: a table
of one row for what an administrator sets about the Assistant's model. Nothing
else to run. The model goes on running on `ASSISTANT_API_KEY`, switched on,
exactly as it did, until somebody changes that in the app.

To keep a key in the app the server needs `AUTH_SESSION_SECRET`, which a
deployment with Microsoft sign-in already has. Without it the switch works and
a key is refused, in words that say what to set. `dev.env` carries one for
trying this on a laptop; it is not a secret, and is no deployment's.

A repo with routes of its own under `/api/assistant/` or `/api/people/` hides
the package's there, as it always has: the consumer's routes are asked first.

Rolling back to 2.6.0 leaves the table, which 2.6.0 does not look at: the model
is back on the deployment's key, switched on, whatever the app had said. A map
saved from 2.6.0 is written without its features' leans, which come back wide.

## How it works

### A feature leans

- **Ctrl with < or >** on a selected feature steps its bubble through the five
  shapes a capability's oval has: wide, a little wide, round, a little tall,
  tall. The same keys, the same scale, and no control in the details panel, as
  a capability has none.
- **Wide is the shape a feature has always had**, and is whatever its row made
  it: the rows are packed for bubbles lying wide, and nothing about them
  changes. A step at a time the bubble's two radii trade places, exactly as a
  capability's do — so round is the mean of the two, and tall is the same
  bubble a quarter turn round.
- A bubble stood up is **taller than its row**, and its neighbours are not
  moved for it. That is what a capability stood up in a crowded domain does to
  the capabilities beside it, and the answer is the same: drag it to where it
  has the room. Left in its row, it is moved only as far as keeps it under the
  capability's name and inside the rim.
- Its **name is its own to size**. Every name in a capability is set at one
  size, worked out from the bubbles as their rows made them; a bubble that
  leans sets its name no larger than that, and smaller where its new shape has
  less width for it. Standing one feature up does not reset the type of the
  rest.
- A bubble a crowded row has already squeezed no wider than it is tall has no
  length to stand up on, and is left as it is.
- The lean is the feature's, and goes with it when it is handed to another
  capability or trades places with another feature.

### The lines of a coarser level

- **A folded line runs between the closest pair of connection points**, as a
  line nobody has anchored does. Each end used to be the point facing the
  other shape's middle, chosen with no regard to the other end. Round a large
  domain, whose twenty-four points are a long way apart, the two could be a
  lobe out of step, and a line with a hand's width to cross set off sideways
  along the boundary.
- Bent round a shape in its way, each end is the point nearest the bend it
  makes for, not the one nearest a far end it no longer runs straight to.
- **A folded line is broken.** A line somebody drew is whole. Both cross a
  boundary and wear the colour that says so, and a fold of one line weighs what
  a line does, so on a map where a domain is joined to a domain by hand the two
  could not be told apart — and only one of them can be taken hold of. The
  breaks go by the line's own width, three widths drawn to two left out and cut
  square, so a heavy fold is not a string of beads nor a light one a row of
  bars. Export SVG draws the same.
- **A connection point no longer falls onto the middle of its domain.** A
  domain's points are found where rays from the middle of its body leave its
  outline. A ray that left exactly through a corner of the outline meets two
  runs of it, each at its very end, and the arithmetic could put it a hair past
  the end of both: no crossing at all, and the point came back as the middle
  itself. A line then ran into the blob and stopped on whatever capability sat
  there. Which rays did depended on the last digits of the outline, so it came
  and went as a lobe was resized.

### A domain's outline

- The outline was sampled along ninety-six rays from the middle of the body,
  the outermost crossing on each. A ray can report one crossing, and under the
  end of a title that hangs off the blob it crosses the surface three times:
  the outline leapt from the far side of the title to the body along the ray,
  and the blob had a bite taken out of it.
- It is **walked** now: from where the surface is met going right from the
  middle, a short step at a time along the surface itself, until the walk is
  back where it began. It goes wherever the surface goes — into a bay and back
  out of it — and is then drawn through points evenly spaced along its length,
  never fewer than the ninety-six there were and more the longer it runs. A
  domain whose outline the rays could follow is the same outline, to within a
  unit.
- A walk that fails — the surface goes flat under it, or it does not come back
  round the middle — falls back to the rays, so the worst a blob can be is what
  it was.
- **A title that hangs off the blob stands on a shoulder.** Its lobe was an
  oval joined to the blob by however much of the two happened to overlap, with
  a bay either side under the ends of the title, deeper the further out it
  went. A title whose middle is off the blob now has its lobe swept back along
  the way to the middle of the body, as far as the last place that is on the
  blob: the title stands on a shoulder as wide as itself. A title whose middle
  is on the blob — every title until it is dragged off — is drawn as it was.

### A line's shape, beside the line

- A selected line in Edit mode carries a small **i** beside its middle: off the
  line, since a curved line has a bend there to take hold of, and on the lower
  side, since the line's name is lifted over the middle when it is pointed at.
- Pressing it, or **I**, opens the two things there are to set about how a line
  is drawn, next to the line: **Curved** or **Straight**, and **Anchor**, which
  keeps the line on the points it was drawn to.
- They are the details panel's own two fields, built by the same function, so
  the pop-up and the panel cannot offer different things, and a change in one
  shows in the other at once. Each change is one step of undo, wherever it was
  made.
- **Curved or Straight is a toggle**: both words in sight, either one press. A
  choice of two behind a list has to be opened to see what the other is.
- The panel's **Shape section starts open for a line**, where a shape's starts
  shut. A shape has a name and an owner that come before how it is drawn; a
  line has neither, and its shape is most of what there is to set.
- The pop-up shuts on I, on Esc — which leaves the line selected — and on a
  press on the map or a turn of the wheel, since the line may be about to move
  from under it. It is not drawn for a line of a hidden layer, and it is not
  there while browsing.

### The Assistant's model, from the app

- **Assistant model**, under the avatar, for an administrator and nobody else.
- **On or Off.** Off, contributors are told of no model, and the Assistant
  writes prompts to copy out by hand, as it does where none was ever connected;
  a message sent anyway is refused in words that say an administrator switched
  it off. It applies at the next message: the setting is read for every request
  that needs it, so there is no restart and no redeploy.
- **A key of the app's own.** Where the model is — `ASSISTANT_API_URL` — and
  what it is called stay the deployment's to say. The key is the part that
  expires, and it can now be given here: it is used in place of
  `ASSISTANT_API_KEY` from the next message on, and a deployment given an
  address and no key is connected by it. **Forget this key** goes back to the
  deployment's.
- **The key goes one way.** It is typed into a box that shows dots, sent once,
  and the box is emptied. No route answers with it — not the settings, not an
  error, not the result of trying it — so there is nothing for the page to show
  but that there is one: a row of eight dots, always eight, with when it was
  set and by whom.
- **It is kept sealed.** Before it is written the key is encrypted with
  AES-256-GCM, under a key derived from `AUTH_SESSION_SECRET` for this one
  purpose. The secret is in the deployment and not in the database, so a copy
  of the table — a backup, a dump handed to someone — holds nothing that can be
  used.
- A server with **no secret** has nothing to seal with, and refuses to keep a
  key rather than keep one in the clear. A server whose **secret has changed**
  cannot open the key it kept: the dialog says so, the model runs on the
  deployment's key until the app's is set again, and nothing is lost that was
  not already unreadable.
- **Try it** asks the model for one word, whatever the switch says, and reports
  whether it took the key — in the API's own words when it did not. Those
  words are passed on with the key taken out of them: some APIs quote a refused
  key back, whole or with its middle starred, and four letters of a secret are
  still four letters of it.
- A key is one run of printable characters, eight to 4096 of them. What a
  paste brings round it is trimmed; a space in the middle, or a placeholder
  nobody pasted over, is refused.
- A model of the consumer's own, handed to `createDomainMapServer` as
  `assistant`, brings whatever key it needs. The switch applies to it; there is
  no key here to change, and the dialog says so.

### Two skills, and what every skill is told

- **Word the lines** rewrites the descriptions on lines that read as steps in a
  flow — *triggers*, *is stored in*, *consumes events*, *checks whether* — as
  what one side provides the other: a promise, or a fact that passes between
  them. A line has two ends and no direction, so a description that says who
  calls whom claims something the line does not, and a map worded that way is
  read as a call graph and argued about as one. It works on one line, a domain
  or the whole map, and may only describe.
- **Question the ownership** looks at the map through its areas. An area says
  who answers for a part of the map today: a fact about the organisation,
  which a reorganisation changes without the business doing anything
  different. So the skill reports where the two cuts pull against each other —
  a domain that leans on another team's, two areas joined by many lines, ground
  nobody holds — and does not propose moving anything to make the map fit the
  teams. Who holds what is the owner's decision; its findings are notes.
- Both rules are in the account of the map **every skill reads**, so the other
  skills write a line's description as a promise too, and none argues for a
  boundary from who owns it. *Question the connections* rewrites a line worded
  as a call when it meets one.
- They began as two notes a model wrote about a map: that areas were being
  read as boundaries, and lines as calls. A note says it once, to whoever asked.

### Users & access

- **Finding someone.** A box looks a person up by name or address as it is
  typed, and two lists narrow the rows by role and by when someone was last
  here: the last 7, 30 or 90 days, not in the last 90, or not yet.
- **A page at a time**, twenty-five to a page, with how many people there are
  and which are on show. Narrowing goes back to the first page.
- It is all done on the page, over the list as it was read: the list is a name
  and a date a person, and the Owner box reads the whole of it anyway.
- **Wider**, 1000px where there is the room, and a long name or address wraps
  inside its own column rather than holding the row on one line. At 760px a row
  with an address in it, a role to choose and a button to press was read by
  scrolling sideways.
- **What each role may do is at the foot.** It is read once and glanced back
  at, and the list is what the dialog is opened for.
- A last sign-in from another year says which year. Without it, someone last
  here thirteen months ago read as here last month.

### The clear-out

- Everyone who signs in once is on the list for good, and 2.5.1's **Remove**
  takes them off one at a time. An administrator can now remove **everyone not
  signed in** for 90 days, 180 days or a year, or since a day of their
  choosing — of any role, or the viewers, contributors or publishers alone.
- **Not signed in** means last here before the day; for someone added by email
  who never came, added before it.
- It counts them as it is set, and its button removes nobody. **Preview…**
  asks the server who it would take, and the list becomes exactly those
  people — each with their role, their sandbox and when they were last here —
  under a bar that says what removing them costs. **Remove** is pressed there,
  or **Cancel**. There is no "are you sure": the list is the question.
- **What was shown is the most that goes.** The removal is handed the ids of
  the people on the list, and takes only those of them who are still away.
  Someone who signs in while the list is being read is spared, and someone who
  crosses the day meanwhile is left for the next time.
- **Administrators are never taken this way**, however long away: a sweep that
  happened to catch them all would leave nobody to run the list. One is
  removed by hand, by another. Nor is whoever asks.
- **Nobody here within the last 30 days is inactive**, whatever day is picked:
  the server refuses a nearer one. A clear-out takes sandboxes with it and
  cannot be undone, and a day a week back, picked by a slip of the hand, would
  take most of the list.
- Each person goes as one removed by hand does: their sandbox and logins with
  them, what they shared left shared, and anyone who can still sign in back as
  a contributor.

### Panels

- The **Collapse** toggle holds itself to the foot of its panel, open or
  folded. It used to lean on what was over it to be held there, and the
  hierarchy panel has only its tree over the foot: folded, the tree is gone,
  and the toggle rose to the top of the rail, away from where it had just been
  pressed.

## What it does not do

- **No rows that make room for a lean.** The rows are packed for bubbles lying
  wide. A capability whose features are all stood up is not repacked.
- **No address or name for the model in the app.** The switch and the key are
  set there; `ASSISTANT_API_URL` and `ASSISTANT_MODEL` are the deployment's.
- **No directory behind the people list.** Finding people is finding the ones
  who have signed in or been added.
- **No sparing one person from a clear-out.** The preview is read, not edited:
  to keep someone on it, cancel, and narrow the clear-out by role or by day —
  or give them another role first.

## Known issues

- A feature stood up or made round lies across its neighbours until it is
  dragged clear.
- A domain still carries twenty-four connection points, set out by direction,
  and round a large one they are far apart: a folded line crosses the gap
  between two neighbours, but not always squarely.
- A folded line shorter than a dash or two reads as whole.
- The pop-up beside a line does not follow the map. It shuts when the map is
  pressed or zoomed, and is opened again where the line now is.
- Changing `AUTH_SESSION_SECRET` leaves a key kept in the app unreadable, as it
  signs everyone out. The key is set again.
- With sign-in off, whoever can reach the server is an administrator, and can
  switch the model or give it a key — as they can do everything else there.
- A clear-out cannot be undone: the versions in the sandboxes it takes are
  gone.
- The example map's own lines are worded as steps in a flow, which is what
  *Word the lines* rewrites.
