# 2.6.0

The map gains a level below the capability, and a way to say how much of
itself to spell out. A capability can now hold **features** — what it does,
named one by one — and it opens onto them as the view closes in. A line may end
on a domain, on a capability or on a feature, in any combination, so one part
of a map can be drawn down to its features while another is left as a domain
that talks to a domain. And a slider beside the zoom says which of those three
levels the lines are drawn at: where they really end, lifted to capabilities,
or lifted to domains, where the lines between two domains become one.

Nothing moves as the view closes in or the level changes. A domain does not
collapse to make an overview, and a capability does not swell to show what it
holds: where a shape sits and how big it is are the map's meaning, so what
changes is only how much is spelt out. And nothing changes when Edit is left:
the level is the same browsing and editing, so the map someone has just saved
is the map they are looking at.

Two more things came with it. **Draw** puts a marker in your hand over a locked
map, so a flow is traced on the terrain itself rather than on a screenshot of
it — the overlay the map was always meant to carry. And the page has a **dark
theme**, which follows the system until someone chooses, with the map drawn in
each swatch's dark counterpart rather than in a second palette.

The map reached by an agent from outside, which this number was first kept
for, has moved on once more: its outline is on [2.7.0](2.7.0.md).

## Breaking changes

| What | Was | Is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Document version | `4` | `5`. A 2.5 deployment refuses a version 5 file. |
| A capability in a map file | its own fields | may also carry `features`, a list written under it, and the size of its lobe |
| What a line at the top of a map file joins | two capabilities | a domain, a capability or a feature at either end, said by `fromKind` and `toKind` where it is not a capability |
| What a connector may end on | a capability, a touchpoint, an actor | a domain and a feature as well |
| Where a curved line nobody has shaped runs | one easy bend between its ends | round a domain or a loose capability that lies between them, where it can |
| A drag that starts on a shape, while browsing | selects the shape and moves nothing | selects it and pans the map |
| How wide a line is drawn | its weight in map units, at every zoom | the same, held between 2.25 and 14 pixels on screen |
| The page on a device set to dark | light | dark, until someone chooses Light under the avatar |
| Export SVG, pressed in the dark theme | — | the dark map on its dark paper; switch to Light first for a slide |
| Export SVG, with a flow drawn on the map | — | carries the drawing |

The file break is one-way, as every one before it was: 2.6 reads every version
1 to 4 file there is, as a map whose capabilities hold nothing, and what a 2.5
deployment cannot do is read a file 2.6 wrote. Keep a 2.5-era export of
anything that has to travel backwards before upgrading.

## Upgrading

Nothing to run: no migration, and no new environment variable. A brand written
before this comes out dark where the system is, without saying anything;
[BRANDING.md](../BRANDING.md#the-dark-theme) has what it may restate.

Stored versions stay as they were written until someone saves them, as with
every file version so far: version 4 in the database, version 5 the next time
it is saved. Nothing is rewritten behind anyone's back.

A map opens drawn as it was. Every line is where it ended, at the level every
map opens at; the one difference anyone will see without touching anything is
that some curved lines now go round a domain they used to cross.

## How it works

### Lines, and what they may join

- The terrain is three kinds, each inside the last: a **domain** holds
  capabilities, a **capability** holds features. A line may join any two of
  them, in any combination — a domain to a domain, a feature to a capability in
  another domain, two features of one capability.
- That is what lets a map be uneven on purpose. Where a part of the business
  is well understood its capabilities are drawn down to their features and the
  lines run between those; where it is not, a domain is joined to a domain and
  nothing is pretended about what inside it does the talking.
- The one line refused is between a shape and the one that holds it: a
  capability and its own domain, a feature and its own capability or domain. The
  one is already part of the other. For the same reason a capability is not
  dropped into a domain it is connected to, nor a feature into a capability:
  the page says to remove the connector first.
- A domain and a feature carry **connection points** as a capability does, the
  same twenty-four directions. A domain's are where those directions leave its
  blob, seen from the middle of its body; a feature shows every other one, and
  smaller, since its bubble is small. A shape shows its own points when it is
  pointed at and not those of what is inside it.
- Lines of the presentation layer are as they were: an actor reaches a
  touchpoint and a touchpoint a capability, and neither reaches a domain or a
  feature.
- A line between two shapes of the terrain is filed under the end it was drawn
  from, in the menu as in the file: a domain's own lines hang under the domain,
  after its capabilities, and a feature's under the feature.
- A line is **internal** when both its ends are in one domain, whatever they
  end on, and cross-domain otherwise. A line that ends on a domain itself
  crosses a boundary by definition.

### The levels

- The **level** is how much of each line is spelt out, and there are three,
  named for what the lines are drawn to: **features**, **capabilities** and
  **domains**. The slider over the zoom buttons chooses, finest at the top, the
  way + is over −; its stops are lettered F, C and D.
- At the feature level every line is drawn where it really ends. It is the
  level a map opens at, because there is nothing to explain about it: what is
  drawn is what was drawn.
- At a coarser level each end of a line is **lifted** to the first shape, going
  up from where it really ends, that the level draws lines to: a feature to its
  capability at the capability level, a capability to its domain at the domain
  level. A line whose ends are both where they belong is drawn as the line it
  is; one with an end lifted is drawn between the places it was lifted to.
- Every line lifted to the same two places is one **folded line**, as heavy as
  what it carries — two and a half pixels for one line, a step for every
  doubling, never more than seven — and it keeps the real lines inside it. One
  that stands for a single line is picked as that line; one that stands for
  several names itself under the pointer, and how many it carries.
- A line whose two ends lift to the same place is inside that place, and at
  that level is not drawn: the lines within a domain are no part of the picture
  of which domain talks to which.
- A fold is not drawn beside a line that really joins the same two places. The
  pair is already shown as joined, by the line someone drew to say so.
- **At the domain level each domain keeps its two strongest folded lines**
  while nothing is picked: the level is for the main roads. A line is drawn
  when either of its ends counts it among its strongest, so no domain that has
  a line is left without one. Heavier first; between two as heavy, the shorter,
  since a line to a neighbour crosses less of the map. On the example, eleven
  cross-domain lines over seven pairs of domains come to five.
- **Picking is how the rest are asked for.** A domain picked shows every line
  it has, and no other domain's. A shape picked that the level would have
  lifted is left where it is, so its lines leave from the shape itself. A line
  picked shows the fold it is in.
- **The level is the same browsing and editing.** A line drawn short of its own
  ends cannot be taken hold of — it has no ends on the stage — so shaping a line
  is done at the level it is drawn whole. But the stage does not change under
  anyone when they press Edit, Save or Cancel.
- The level is the tab's, like a layer hidden while browsing: no part of the
  map, never saved, and back at the feature level the next time the page opens.
- The zoom does not choose the level. It decides only what there is to end a
  line on: a feature is on the stage once its capability has opened, and until
  then a line to it leaves from the capability, at any level.
- Lines of the presentation layer are drawn as they are at every level.

### Going round what is in the way

- A line says that two things are joined, and says it best over open ground.
  Drawn straight across a third shape it reads as joined to that as well, or
  hides its name. So a line that would cross a domain, or a capability that
  sits in no domain, is bent round it.
- It is a leaning and not a law. The bend is put beside the first shape
  crossed, on its nearer side and clear of it by about half the ground two
  domains keep between them, and each half of the line is looked at again, a
  few turns deep. A shape too large to go round — further out of the way than
  three quarters of the line's own length — is not gone round, and the line
  runs straight.
- It applies to the lines the page routes: every folded line, and a curved
  line nobody has shaped. A line bent by hand keeps its bends, and a line set
  to straight stays straight. The domains a line's own ends are in are never in
  its way, since it has to cross them to get out.

### Features

- A **feature** is one thing a capability does, named. It carries a title, a
  description, an owner and a Type, as every shape does, and a Type list of its
  own. It belongs to exactly one capability: it is on whatever layer and in
  whatever area its capability is.
- It has no size and no colour. It is drawn inside its capability's oval, in
  rows, in the order the capability holds it — or where in the oval it has been
  put by hand.
- A capability holds forty-eight at most. Past that they are set too small for
  any zoom to read comfortably, and a capability with more than that to say is
  usually two.
- **Add a feature** sits with *Add a domain* and *Add a capability* on the
  Domains layer, and follows the selection as they do: into the capability
  that is picked, or beside the feature that is. With neither picked the button
  is there and says what it is waiting for. The new feature lands last, is
  picked so its title can be typed at once, and the map closes in on its
  capability — from any further out there would be nothing to show for the
  press.
- **In Edit mode a feature is dragged, as a capability is.** Let go inside its
  own capability it stays where it was put, held within the rim. Let go on
  another feature the two trade places. Let go in another capability it is
  handed over — to where it was dropped if that capability is open, to the end
  of its list if it is shut. Anywhere else it goes back: a feature is always in
  a capability.
- A place given by hand is kept in hundredths of the oval's two radii, so a
  feature keeps its place when the capability is resized. *Put back in its
  row*, in the feature's details, returns it to the row its order gives it.
- `]` and `[` move the picked feature to the end of its capability's list or to
  the start, and with Ctrl one place later or earlier: the keys that restack a
  capability.
- In the menu a capability's features hang under it, before its lines: what it
  is made of, then where it reaches. In the details panel a capability says how
  many it holds and lists them as chips, and pressing one closes the map in on
  it; a feature's own panel is its fields and the capability it is in, and has
  no Shape section, since it has no shape to set.
- Deleting a feature takes its lines. Deleting a capability takes its features
  and their lines, deleting a domain takes all of that for every capability in
  it, and undo brings every one back.

### Opening

- A capability **opens** once its features' names would be drawn ten pixels
  high on screen, and shuts again as the view pulls back. Until then the names
  would be a texture inside the oval, and the capability's own title, which
  they take the room of, says more.
- So how close it takes depends on what is inside. The features are packed in
  rows that follow the oval — longer through its middle than at its top and
  foot — in as many rows as leaves the tightest bubble the most room, and every
  name in one capability is set at one size: three features open sooner than
  thirty. One very long name is cut with an ellipsis before it is allowed to
  shrink all the others.
- Shut, a capability that holds features wears a row of pips along its foot,
  one a feature up to seven. They are small enough to be gone from the whole
  map and to show as it closes in, which is when "there is more in here" is
  worth saying.
- Open, its own name moves up into the crown of the oval, set small, and its
  icon stands down. Each feature is a bubble of white laid over the
  capability's colour, so it wears that colour paled and needs none of its own.
- The features are drawn over the lines. A line to a feature in the middle of
  its capability has to cross the ones beside it to get out, and drawn over
  them it struck through their names; drawn under them it passes behind, and a
  press on a bubble lands on the bubble.
- Capabilities overlap, a platform in the middle of a domain most of all. One
  that is open and picked — itself, or a feature in it — is painted over its
  neighbours for as long as it is. Nothing is restacked.
- **The map zooms in as far as its features need.** The limit was four times,
  and still is for a map with no features; one whose smallest features are set
  smaller than that can read is given what it takes, up to twenty-four times.
- **Double-click** a domain or a capability to glide in to it. A domain is
  fitted to the stage. A capability is brought to where it can be taken in
  among its neighbours and, where it holds features, as close as reading them
  comfortably takes — though not so close that its oval runs off the stage.
  Double-clicking a domain's *title* still renames it. **Fit to screen** glides
  back out, and anyone who has asked their system for less motion gets the jump
  instead.

### A domain's lobes

- A domain's blob is drawn round its capabilities: each has a **lobe**, the
  capability's oval and a ring of blob round it, and the outline is what joins
  them up. The ring was one size for every lobe. It is now each capability's
  own.
- **In Edit mode the boundary of a selected domain is a handle.** Press it and
  the lobe behind that stretch of boundary is the one taken hold of; drag out
  and its ring widens until the boundary passes under the pointer, drag in and
  it narrows. The capability stays the size and in the place it is.
- This is for room to work in. A capability dragged about its domain leaves it
  when it goes too far past the blob, and in a domain drawn tight that is not
  far; a lobe pulled out makes ground to move things over. It is also a way to
  give a domain a shape — a bay beside a capability for another to move into.
- A ring is never less than a quarter of the usual one, so a capability is
  always inside its lobe, and never more than twelve times it. *Reset shapes*
  puts every lobe in the domain back to the usual ring.

### Getting about

- **Browsing, a drag pans from wherever it starts.** Outside Edit mode nothing
  on the map can be moved, so a drag that starts on a domain or a capability
  moves the map as one on open ground does, and a click still picks the shape.
  Zoomed in on a capability there is no open ground left to start a drag on,
  which is what made this necessary. In Edit mode a drag on a shape moves the
  shape, as it did.
- **A line keeps a width that can be seen.** A line's weight is in map units,
  so with the whole map in view it thinned to a hair, and zoomed far in it
  swelled into a road. What is drawn is now held between two widths on screen —
  2.25 pixels and 14, with a picked line never under 4.5 so that it still reads
  as picked.
- **And a target that can be hit.** The band round a line that takes the
  pointer is measured on screen as well: twenty pixels, forty under a finger.
  With the whole map in view on a phone it used to be about three.

### Drawing on the map

The map is the terrain and a flow is a route across it. Until now the route was
drawn somewhere else — on a printout, or over a screenshot in another tool.
**Draw** puts the marker on the map itself, and keeps the two things apart: the
terrain is the map's, saved in versions and the same for everyone; a drawing is
one conversation's, and belongs to the tab it was made in.

#### A mode of its own

- **Draw** is a switch in the header, like Assistant. While it is on the map is
  locked: a press never reaches a shape, so nothing is selected, moved or named
  under the marker, and a drag that would have panned the map draws on it.
- Draw and Edit are never on together. A marker is for a map that holds still,
  and Edit is left by Save or Cancel, which is a decision Draw has no business
  making for anyone — so the Draw chip leaves the row while Edit is on, and
  going into Edit puts the marker down. Leaving Draw costs nothing: what was
  drawn stays on the map, and the map answers the pointer again through it.
- It needs no role. Nothing of a drawing goes to the server, so a viewer draws
  as freely as a contributor.
- The map still moves under a marker. The wheel and a pinch zoom as they always
  did; Space with a drag, the middle button, or two fingers pan. A second
  finger landing drops the stroke the first had begun, so a pinch on a phone
  never leaves a dot behind.

#### Strokes

- A stroke is held in the map's own units and painted in a layer of its own
  inside the viewport, above every layer of the map. It rides the terrain
  through pan and zoom, and nothing has to follow the view to keep it there.
- A marker is 6px wide on screen (`--marker-width`) at whatever zoom a stroke
  is made, and the stroke keeps that width on the map: drawn zoomed in it is a
  fine line, drawn zoomed out a broad one, and from then on it thins and
  thickens with the shapes it was drawn over, the way a line on a paper map
  does.
- Five markers: red, orange, green, blue and black. They are the saturated
  ends of the raw colour scales, where the palette is pale, so a route reads
  against any fill and against the grey of the connectors. A stroke names its
  marker rather than carrying a colour, so restating `--marker-*` in a theme
  or a brand recolours what has already been drawn.
- When a stroke is let go, its points are thinned to the ones the line needs —
  a straight run comes down to its two ends — moving it by less than a pixel
  at the zoom it was drawn at. The line through them bends at each point rather
  than turning there, and where two long runs meet it rounds the corner by no
  more than the marker is wide. So it looks drawn by hand, an arrowhead keeps
  its point, and a stroke does not change shape under the pointer as it is let
  go.
- A press that goes nowhere is a dot.

#### The eraser, undo and clear

- The eraser takes whole strokes: press one, or drag across several. A flow is
  redrawn, not trimmed. It reaches 10px round a pointer, shown as a ring, and
  18px round a finger, which covers what it is aiming at.
- One drag of the eraser is one step back, however many strokes it crossed, and
  so is **Clear** — which is why Clear does not ask first. A stroke put back
  goes back under the ones drawn over it.
- Ctrl+Z is the drawing's own undo while Draw is on; the map's has nothing to
  undo outside Edit. It reaches back a hundred steps.
- Esc drops the stroke in hand, or else puts the marker down. **Done**, on the
  tray, puts it down without a keyboard and without a trip back along the
  header, which on a phone is a swipe away.

#### What is kept, and what is not

- A drawing is not part of the map. No version holds it, Export does not write
  it, the map file's shape is unchanged, and nobody else sees it.
- It is kept in the tab's session storage, as unsaved edits are and for the
  same reason: a refresh in the middle of a conversation, or the trip out to
  sign in again, should not wipe the board, while a second tab on the same map
  starts clean. The steps back are kept with it. Closing the tab lets it go.
- It belongs to the terrain it was drawn on. Opening another version in the
  tab, or importing a file, wipes it, and a tab pointed at another version's
  address does not get the last one's drawing laid over it.
- **Export SVG** carries it. The picture is what is on screen, and a flow over
  the terrain is as often as not the reason the picture is wanted: the strokes
  go in after the map's layers, each with its colour and width written onto it.

### A dark theme

The page has two themes, and the map is drawn in either. Dark is not the light
page inverted: the chrome is the same tokens said again for dark paper, and the
map keeps its one palette and is drawn in each swatch's *dark counterpart* —
the same colour in less light. No environment variable, no migration, and map
files are untouched: which theme someone is looking in is no part of a map.

#### Who decides

- The theme follows the system until someone chooses: dark when the device is,
  light when it is not, and it turns with the device while the page is open.
- **Theme**, in the profile popup under the avatar, offers **System**, **Light**
  and **Dark**. The one in force is filled in, as the palette's size is. It
  sits with who you are because it is yours and not the map's: two people
  looking at the same version need not be looking at it in the same light.
- The choice is kept by the browser, in `localStorage` beside which panels are
  folded, under `domain-map:theme`. Another tab of the same browser follows it
  at once. Choosing System keeps nothing.
- The sign-in screen has no control of its own and follows the same choice, so
  it does not open white on a dark desk.
- The page opens in its theme. `js/theme-first.js` is the one script either
  page loads the old way — in the head, ahead of the stylesheets, not as a
  module — because a module runs after the first paint and a dark page would
  open on a flash of white every time. It puts `data-theme` on the root and
  does nothing else; `js/theme.js` takes over once the page is up. It is served
  before sign-in, with the sign-in screen's own script and styles.

#### How the page turns

- `tokens.css` is still the source of truth. The raw scales are the same in
  both themes; the dark theme is one block that says again what each semantic
  token is, under `:root[data-theme="dark"]`. Nothing in `layout.css`,
  `diagram.css` or `login.css` knows there are two themes.
- One selector, not that block written twice — once there and once under a
  `prefers-color-scheme` query. Without a build step that is two lists to keep
  in step, and two a brand would have to restate. The script resolves what the
  system prefers and writes the answer where the stylesheet reads it.
- The colours that were written into the stylesheets are tokens now, since a
  colour written anywhere else is one the dark theme cannot reach: `--error`
  and `--error-ink` for what went wrong, `--ink-strong` for what is being
  typed, `--line-quiet` for a note's edge, `--swatch-edge`, `--scrim` behind a
  dialog and `--shadow-weight` under one. The light theme draws what it drew:
  the same view of the map, before and after, is the same picture pixel for
  pixel.
- A new raw scale, `night`, is to the dark theme what sage and charcoal are to
  the light one: the chrome's surfaces and lines, tinted toward the brand green
  as sage is, and the two inks read on them. The stage is the darkest surface
  where it was the lightest, and the chrome a shade lighter where it was a
  shade darker, so the map is still what the page is built round.
- The selection steps up to a lighter blue. The light theme's is as bright as a
  blue can be and still carry white type, and against dark paper that is a dull
  line; so on dark it is `#4a9bff`, and `--selection-ink` — new — says what is
  written on a control filled with it, which is white in one theme and dark in
  the other. `--ink-invert` stays white in both: the fills it is read on, the
  brand colour and the orange of unsaved work, are the same fills.
- A shadow is still black, and there is three times as much of it. Next to
  nothing shows against a page that is already dark.

#### How the map turns

- **The palette is the map's, not the theme's.** `--c1` … `--c24` are copied
  into a map file the moment its palette is edited, so they have to mean the
  same thing whatever the theme. What changes is how a swatch is drawn.
- **A dark counterpart is the same colour in less light.** Every channel is
  dimmed by the same share, as light rather than as the number in the hex, so
  the hue and the saturation are exactly what they were: a yellow becomes that
  yellow in a darker room, not a brown chosen to stand in for it. A colour
  keeps 27% of its own brightness on top of a floor, which brings the pale end
  of a palette down by half and leaves it the pale end.
- **It only ever dims.** A swatch already deep enough for dark paper is left as
  it is — eight of the stock twenty-four. Lifted to match the rest, a dark red
  would be drawn paler on the dark map than on the light one.
- **It is worked out, not stored.** A second palette would be a second thing
  for every map and every brand to keep; worked out from the swatch, a map
  whose palette has been edited and a brand that recoloured `--c1` years ago
  come out dark without either having to say anything. A stylesheet that wants
  a say names `--c1-dark` … `--c24-dark`, and what it names holds for its own
  swatch: once a map has made that swatch another colour, the counterpart named
  for the old one is no longer its counterpart.
- **A capability's title is still black.** The floor is where black type starts
  to read at 4.5 to 1, so nothing that carried a black title on the light map
  is taken below it and a label is the same label on every swatch, in both
  themes. That is also why the counterparts are dusk rather than midnight: an
  icon is drawn as its file has it, in the colours whoever drew it chose, and
  an icon drawn to hold against the light palette holds against fills that are
  still light enough for black type. Fills dark enough for white type would
  have swallowed every icon on the map.
- **A see-through shape is the exception.** Its fill lets the paper through,
  and on a dark map the paper is dark: an actor starts at half. Black stays for
  as long as it reads at 3 to 1 — what large type needs, and a title on the map
  is never small — and turns white past that. An actor's figure goes with its
  name. On the light map nothing changes: the title is black whatever the shape.
- **Titles on the paper are inked for the paper**, as they always were. A
  domain's is black or white by what is under it, and under a 20% wash on dark
  paper that is white. An area's wears its border's colour made readable, which
  on dark paper means lifted towards the light ink rather than deepened towards
  the dark one.
- **The lines swap ends of the scale and keep their meaning.** A public line is
  still the one that stands out from the paper, now by being light and fine; an
  internal one still sinks towards it, broad and dim; a presentation line is
  the same teal, lifted.
- **Edit palette edits the palette**, which is the colours as the light theme
  draws them. On a dark page the swatches in the editor and the shapes behind
  it are therefore not the same colours, and a line in the editor says so —
  there, and only there.
- **Export SVG draws what is on screen**, and the theme is part of that: from
  the dark theme the picture is the dark map on its dark paper, readable
  wherever it is put. For a slide or a wall, switch to Light first. The other
  way — always the light map — would have meant redrawing the stage in another
  theme behind the reader's back for as long as the fonts take to embed.

#### A brand

- A brand written before this says what its light tokens are and nothing else.
  It comes out dark all the same: the dark block outranks a plain `:root`, so
  the brand's light paper does not win there and leave the page half of each.
  The brand colour still goes where the brand colour went — the badge, the
  avatar, the edge of a card — and the selected row is mixed from it.
- What a brand may restate, and how a page of its own wears the theme, is in
  [BRANDING.md](../BRANDING.md#the-dark-theme).

### The file

Version 5. A capability's features are written under it, in order, and only
when it holds any; one put somewhere by hand carries a `shape` with its place,
in hundredths of the capability's radii. A capability whose lobe has been
dragged carries `lobe` in its own `shape`.

```json
{
  "key": "invoicing",
  "domain": "billing",
  "title": "Invoicing",
  "shape": { "position": "120,40", "color": 3, "lobe": 2.4 },
  "features": [
    { "key": "issue-an-invoice", "title": "Issue an invoice", "description": "From an order, once it ships." },
    { "key": "credit-note", "title": "Credit note", "shape": { "position": "-40,25" } }
  ]
}
```

A feature's key is its own across the whole file, not only within its
capability, which is what lets a line name one. A line at the top of the file
joins two capabilities unless it says otherwise, so a file with no lines to
domains or features is written exactly as it was:

```json
"connectors": [
  { "from": "invoicing", "to": "collections" },
```

### The example map

Unchanged, so it holds no features and none of its lines ends on a domain: it
opens as it always has.

### Under the hood

`app/js/zoom.js` holds the decisions — where each end of a line is drawn at a
level, the fold, what the domain level keeps, when a capability opens, where a
glide stops — pure, with `tests/zoom.test.mjs` beside it. The feature layout,
the points round a blob, the lobe's ring and the route round a shape are in
`geometry.js`; the glide's path is in `viewport.js`. `diagram.js` reads all of
it after every layout and draws what it says, and hands the zoom to the
stylesheet in `--k`, which is what the line widths are held with.

A drawing is `app/js/strokes.js`, the maths, with `tests/strokes.test.mjs`, and
`app/js/draw.js`, the mode: it takes the stage from `diagram.js` through one
hook, so a press while a marker is in hand never reaches the map's own
handling. The theme is `app/js/theme-first.js` before the first paint and
`app/js/theme.js` after it, and the dark counterpart of a swatch is worked out
in `geometry.js`.

## What it does not do

- **No lines from a touchpoint to a domain or a feature.** A channel reaches a
  capability, as it did.
- **No collapsed domains.** The domain level is the same shapes in the same
  places with the lines lifted; a domain is never redrawn smaller, and keeps
  one position.
- **No level kept with the map.** The slider is the tab's. A map cannot say
  which level it would like to be opened at.
- **Nothing for the Assistant.** The brief a model is shown does not list
  features, nor the lines that end on one, so it is not told what a capability
  does and cannot suggest any.
- **No marks but a freehand stroke.** A drawing has no arrowheads, numbers or
  text of its own.

## Known issues

- A line is bent round domains and loose capabilities only. It may still cross
  a capability inside a domain, and a feature's line crosses the features
  between it and the rim of its capability, passing behind them.
- Round a domain that is hemmed in on every side a bend can land in the
  neighbour it was avoiding, and the line then crosses that instead.
- A folded line that stands for several cannot be picked, so the lines inside
  it are reached by moving the slider up, by picking a shape at one of its
  ends, or from the menu.
- A domain's boundary takes the pointer for resizing only between its
  connection points, which sit on the same outline.
- A stroke does not follow the shapes under it. Move a domain in Edit and what
  was drawn across it stays where it was drawn.
- A logo drawn in a dark colour is hard to see on the dark header. The mark is
  a stored file the page cannot recolour; one that holds on both grounds is the
  fix, and the stock mark does.
- An icon on a see-through capability sits on dark paper showing through, and
  one drawn in dark lines is lost there. The title turns white; the icon
  keeps the colours it was drawn in.
- The sign-in screen takes its theme when it loads, and does not turn if the
  device does while it is open.
