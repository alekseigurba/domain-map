# 2.8.0

2.7.0 let a map say who leads an area or a domain. This release takes the
people one step further down, to the **capability** — which is often where a
product manager and a tech lead are actually named — and gives them a view of
their own: **Roles**, a grid of every area, domain and capability against
every role, for product and for engineering. The cards on the map show who
leads a part of it; the grid shows all of it at once, so a gap is an empty
cell rather than a card that is not there.

The cards themselves moved, from outside the shapes to over them, and a
person's Name box offers the people list again.

The map reached by an agent from outside has moved on once more: its outline
is on [2.9.0](2.9.0.md).

## Breaking changes

| What | Was | Is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Document version | `6` | `7`. A 2.7 deployment refuses a version 7 file. |
| A person in a map file | names the `areas` and `domains` they own | may also name `capabilities`, by their keys, a card moved by hand as `{ key, x, y }` as elsewhere |

## Upgrading

Nothing to run: no migration, and no new environment variable. Stored versions
stay as they were written until someone saves them — version 6 in the
database, version 7 the next time it is saved — and open drawn as they were,
but with each person's cards in their new places. A card someone moved by hand
is where they put it.

Rolling back to 2.7.0 is safe until a version is saved here. One saved since
is a version 7 file, which 2.7.0 will not open.

## How it works

### A capability's leads

- **A person may own a capability**, as they may an area or a domain: on the
  same two layers, with the same card, the same icon and the same role. A
  feature has no lead of its own; it is its capability's.
- Select a capability and *Add a product role* or *Add an engineering role*;
  or name someone from the **Product roles** and **Engineering roles**
  sections of its details, which a capability now has as an area and a domain
  do.
- A person's **Owns** lists their capabilities after their areas and domains,
  each as *name — its domain*: two domains may each have a capability of one
  name, and the chip has to say which.
- The slide of a capability names who leads it, as an area's and a domain's
  slides already did.
- A capability deleted takes nobody with it. Someone who owned only that is
  drawn nowhere and not written to the file, as someone who owned only a
  domain is, and undo brings both back.

### Where a card is

- **A card lies over the shape it names.** It used to hang outside — beyond a
  domain's edge, by its title — which put it further from what it named and,
  on a domain whose title rides the top, straight on to the name of the area
  around it. A map is mostly read with its lower layers let through, so a
  card over the edge of a shape hides nothing that matters, and over the
  shape it reads as that shape's.
- **What a card never covers is words.** On a capability it lies under the
  title and the icon, over the lower part of the oval and past it, growing
  down. On a domain it goes **beside the title**, in whichever of four places
  covers least: over the title, to either side of it, or under it. Each is
  weighed by what the cards would lie on — the title's words most of all, an
  area's name and the capabilities next, and last how much of the cards would
  hang off the domain. No one place suits every domain: over the title is
  clear on one whose title sits low and on to an area's name on one whose
  title rides the top; under it is clear only where the capabilities leave
  room.
- **A card on a capability is smaller**, its name at 24 against a domain's 56:
  the oval is smaller than a domain and sits among others, and at a domain's
  size one card would be wider than the capability it names.
- Everything else is as it was: several people on a shape stack as one
  column, product nearest; cards of two shapes that would meet are moved
  apart; a card moved by hand stays where it was put, measured from its
  shape, and **Put cards back** hands it to the map again.

### Roles

- **Roles**, in the header beside Teams, opens the grid over the page. It is
  everyone's: a viewer reads who leads what as anyone does.
- **Two tabs, Product and Engineering** — the two layers of people, one at a
  time, since a role belongs to one of them.
- **A row is an area, a domain or a capability**, in the order the slideshow
  reads them: an area, its domains, their capabilities, then what is in no
  area. A row is indented by how deep it sits, and an area's and a domain's
  rows are shaded, so the grid reads in its sections. **Find** narrows the
  rows and keeps the ones a found row sits in, so a capability is read under
  its domain.
- **A column is a role**: the discipline's roles as the map lists them, then
  any role someone holds that is off the list, then *No role yet* while
  somebody is in it. Nobody named on the map is left out of the grid.
- **A cell is the people of that role who own that shape.** In Edit mode each
  has a × that takes the shape from them, and a box to type a name into. The
  names already on the map and the people list are offered as you type. Enter,
  or a name picked from the list, puts them in the cell: someone of that name
  already on the map **in that role** is given the shape too, so a person is
  one card a shape and one entry in the file however many cells they are typed
  into; anyone else is added to the map in that role, wearing the icon the
  others in it wear. The same name in another role is another person on the
  map, as it is in the details.
- *Add a role…*, after the last column, adds one to the discipline's list.
- The grid holds nothing of its own. Every change made in it is the change
  the details panel would make, with the same undo, and the map behind it
  redraws as it is made. Outside Edit mode, **Edit** in the grid turns editing
  on for the page.

### The people list in a Name box

A person's **Name** has the people list behind it, as the Owner box has: the
people who have signed in to the map, through Entra ID or otherwise, offered
as the name is typed. It only offered them once an Owner box had been opened
somewhere first, since that was what read the list; a person picked straight
off the map offered nobody. The Name box reads the list for itself now.

## What it does not do

- **No lead for a feature.** It is its capability's.
- **No people from the directory.** The names offered are the people who have
  signed in or been added in Users & access, not everyone in Entra ID. A
  directory behind the list is still on the backlog.
- **No link between a person on the map and a person on the list**, as
  before: a name is typed, and the list only offers.
- **No moving someone between roles from the grid.** Take them out of one
  cell and type them into the other, or change their **Role** in the details.

## Known issues

- A domain's cards are as large as they were, and on a small domain the
  place that covers least may still be mostly off it. Dragging a card is the
  answer, as it was.
- A capability's cards can lie over the capability under it, or over a line:
  they are kept clear of other cards and of areas' names, and of nothing else
  on a neighbour.
