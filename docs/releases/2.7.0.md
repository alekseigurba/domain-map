# 2.7.0

The map has always said what a business does and how its parts sit beside one
another. This release lets it say who is there. **Two layers of people** name
who leads an area or a domain, for product and for engineering, each person a
card hung on what they own. **Teams** say who works on what, and the map can
be lit up by any one of them. Neither is a shape and neither changes where
anything sits: a map is still drawn from what the business does, and the
people are laid over it, to be shown, dimmed or put away like any other layer.

Two more things came with it. **Slideshow** is the map as a deck — a postcard
for every area, domain and capability, made of the map and so never out of
step with it — to work on or to present. And a **shared version can be shared
with a few**, not only with everyone, which needed groups to be practical: the
app now has its own, made from the people already on its list.

The header gave up a third of its buttons on the way. What is set up or looked
up, as against done to the map, is behind a **gear** beside the avatar.

The map reached by an agent from outside, which this number was kept for, has
moved on once more: its outline is on [2.9.0](2.9.0.md).

## Breaking changes

| What | Was | Is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Document version | `5` | `6`. A 2.6 deployment refuses a version 6 file. |
| The layers of a map | three: Areas, Domains, Presentation | five: **Engineering roles** and **Product roles** above them |
| The foot of a map file | the lines | may also carry `teams`, `productRoles` and `engineeringRoles` |
| An area, a domain or a capability in a map file | its `shape` as it was | the `shape` may say what its slide shows, as `postcard`, and name a `picture` |
| `types` in a map file | a list a kind of shape | also one for `team`, `productRole` and `engineeringRole` |
| The file store | `data/icons/`, the logo, the settings | `data/pictures/` as well, for the pictures uploaded for slides |
| A shared version | every contributor's to see | everyone's, or the people and groups it was shared with — and then nobody else's |
| Publishing a shared version | any of them | only one shared with everyone |
| **Share**, in Versions | a yes-or-no question | a dialog that asks who with, opening on *Everyone* |
| `POST /api/drafts/<name>/share` | a name, or nothing | may also carry `audience` |
| `PUT /api/versions/<name>/audience` | not there | its sharer's, and an administrator's |
| `/api/groups` and below | not there | a contributor's to read, an administrator's to change |
| **Export**, **Export SVG**, **Import**, **Getting around** | chips in the header | rows in **Settings**, behind the gear |
| **Theme**, **Users & access**, **Assistant model** | under the avatar | rows in **Settings** |
| The pips on a shut capability | the title's ink at 30% | half as large again, at 60% |

The file break is one-way, as every one before it was: 2.7 reads every version
1 to 5 file there is, as a map with no teams and nobody named, and what a 2.6
deployment cannot do is read a file 2.7 wrote. Keep a 2.6-era export of
anything that has to travel backwards before upgrading.

## Upgrading

One migration, `006-groups.sql`, applied at startup like the others: the
groups and who is in them, and who each shared version is shared with. Nothing
else to run, and no new environment variable.

Every version shared before this is shared with everyone, which is what
sharing meant: the column that says otherwise starts false. Nobody loses sight
of anything by upgrading.

Stored versions stay as they were written until someone saves them: version 5
in the database, version 6 the next time it is saved. A map opens drawn as it
was, with two more rows in its layer control and nothing on either.

The five figures a person's card may wear are files of the package's own,
under `app/icons/people/`, so they need no seeding and are there in a
deployment whose icon library was filled long ago.

A repo with routes of its own under `/api/groups` hides the package's there,
as it always has: the consumer's routes are asked first. A brand that shadows
`index.html` — which is not on the list of what may be shadowed — has the
header's old buttons and none of the new dialogs.

Rolling back to 2.6.1 leaves the tables, which 2.6.1 does not look at: every
shared version is every contributor's again, **including the ones that were
shared with a few**. Take those out of Shared before going back. A version
saved since the upgrade is a version 6 file, which 2.6.1 will not open.

## How it works

### Two layers of people

- **Product roles** and **Engineering roles** are layers like the other three,
  at the top of the stack, with the same eye and dim in the layer control. The
  map is read for product alone, for engineering alone, with both or with
  neither, and nothing under them moves either way.
- A person is on one of the two, and is **a name, a role, an icon and what
  they own**: areas and domains, any number of each. Not capabilities — who
  leads is said where a map is cut into parts somebody answers for, and a
  capability's people are its domain's.
- **Nothing is drawn for a person but their cards**, one on each thing they
  own. A person who owns nothing is nowhere on the map, so one left owning
  nothing is taken off it, and a file does not write them. Undo brings them
  back.
- **A role is typed in once and picked after.** Each layer keeps its own list
  of roles — *Product lead*, *Product manager*; *Engineering lead*, *Tech
  lead*, *Architect* — exactly as a kind of shape keeps its Types: typed into
  the box, on the list from then on, and taken off it again only when nobody
  holds it. The lists are the map's and are written with it.
- A name is typed, and the box offers the people on the app's own list as it
  is typed, as an Owner box does. Nothing ties the two together: a person on
  the map is words, and need never have signed in.
- **Adding one.** In Edit mode, with one of the two layers picked and an area
  or a domain selected, *Add a product role* or *Add an engineering role*; or
  from the shape's own details, which list who leads it for each and offer
  someone already on the map or someone new.

### A person's card

- **The icon on the left, and beside it the name with the role under it.** The
  icon is exactly as tall as the two lines and the air between them, so the
  picture and the words are one block with one top and one foot.
- The name is set at 56 in the map's own units, between a capability's title
  and a domain's: who leads a domain reads from the distance the domain does,
  and is still not louder than what they lead.
- **A band down the card's leading edge is its layer's colour**: warm for
  product, cool for engineering. A product lead and an engineering lead on
  one domain are otherwise the same card, and from any distance a name is the
  last thing read. The two rows in the layer control wear the same band,
  which is where the colours are learnt.
- **The card is paper let through**, at 72%, with the quietest rim the map
  has. It lies over the map, so there is enough of it to read a name against a
  line or a domain's colour, and little enough that what is under it is still
  to be seen.
- **Five figures come with the app** — three women and two men — and a
  person's icon is one of those or anything from the icon library. Someone
  given a role takes the icon the last holder of that role wears, so a variant
  is picked once a role and changed only for whoever is to be told apart.
  Until one is picked the card wears a plain figure, head and shoulders.
- An icon is fitted by its ink, as on a shape, so two drawn with different
  margins are two people of one size.
- Pressing a card picks the person, whose details say what else they own, and
  a person has a link of their own like any shape.

### Where a card is

- **The map hangs a card outside the shape, by its name**: beyond an area's
  title, on the side away from the band, and off a domain's edge on the side
  its title sits. It covers nothing of the shape it is on.
- Several people on one shape are **one column**, stacked away from it,
  product nearest, all as wide as the widest so the icons stand in a line. A
  layer that is hidden gives up its places and the other closes up.
- Two shapes that sit close hang their people in the same place. The cards of
  one are **moved further out** until they are clear of the other's — the
  domains' first, so an area's people are the ones to step out — and clear of
  an area's name, which rides the border just where a domain inside it hangs
  its own. Only ever further out: sideways would slide a card off the side
  its shape's name is on.
- **That is only where a card starts.** In Edit mode a card is dragged like a
  shape, and stays where it is put: kept as a distance from the shape it is
  on — a domain's own point, an area's name — so it goes with the domain when
  the domain is moved, and with the area's name as the area grows.
- A card that was put somewhere leaves the column, which closes up behind it,
  and nothing the map hangs steps out of its way: whoever put it there can see
  what it is over.
- Someone who owns three things has three cards, each put where it reads
  best. **Put cards back**, in the person's details, hands theirs back to the
  map. A move is one step of undo.
- **Fit to screen holds the cards** with everything else, and Export SVG
  carries them, icons and all.

### Teams

- A team is **a name, a description, an owner and a Type** from a list of the
  map's own — product team, enabling team, mission team, whatever the
  organisation calls them — and it is put on what it works on: an area, a
  domain, a capability, or several of them across the map, which is how a
  team that belongs to no one domain is said.
- **Nothing is drawn for a team.** A team is people, and a map with forty of
  them has no room for forty more shapes. A team is read from the things it
  is on.
- The details of an area, a domain or a capability list its **Teams**: the
  ones on it, and after them, quieter, the ones it has through the domain or
  the area that holds it. In Edit mode the same list puts another team on it,
  or makes a new one that starts there.

### The map lit up by a team

- **Teams**, in the header, is a switch like Draw: it puts a legend of the
  teams in the top right corner, under their Types, each with how many shapes
  it is on.
- **Press a team and the map shows it.** What the team is on is ringed; what
  it reaches through that — the capabilities of its domain, everything in its
  area — is left as it is drawn; and the rest stands back, lines and all. The
  people's cards stand back with the rest, since who leads a thing is not the
  subject.
- **Press a Type** and every team of it is lit together: the map by what sort
  of team works on it.
- A lit team stays lit while shapes are picked, so what it is on can be read
  one by one. Pressing it again, or putting the legend away, gives the map
  back.
- It is everyone's, a viewer's too, and needs no Edit mode. In Edit mode the
  legend is also where a team is added.

### Slideshow

- **The deck is made of the map**, in the order the map is read: an area, then
  the domains in it, each followed by its capabilities; and after the areas,
  whatever is in none, the same way. There is nothing to keep in step — a
  domain added is a slide added — and no deck to save.
- **A slide is a postcard**: the shape as the map draws it, beside its words —
  its name, what it is, its owner, who leads it for product and for
  engineering, its teams, what it holds and what it is joined to. A slide says
  only what there is something to say about.
- **Across the top is where the shape sits**, as */area/domain/capability*, so
  a slide met in the middle of a presentation says what it is a part of.
- It opens on the slide of whatever is selected.
- **Edit** is the deck with the slide's title, description, owner and Type as
  fields. They are the shape's own: a word changed on a slide is a word
  changed on the map, undone and saved as any other.
- **Present** is the slide and nothing else, full screen where the browser
  allows it. The arrow keys, Space or a press step through, Home and End go to
  either end, and Esc comes back to the deck. A viewer presents the published
  map as anyone does.

### A slide's picture

- Each slide chooses what it shows of its shape: **the shape** alone, the
  shape **with its neighbours** and the lines between them, or a picture
  **uploaded** for it — a screenshot of the thing the shape stands for, a
  photograph, a sketch.
- An uploaded picture is up to 5 MB, shown whole and never cropped, and kept
  under `data/pictures/`, apart from the icons, so the icon picker does not
  fill with photographs.
- The map keeps the picture's name, as it keeps an icon's. Going back to the
  drawing keeps the name too, so returning to the picture is one press and
  not another upload.

### A version shared with a few

- **Share asks who with**: everyone who works on the map, or the people and
  the groups ticked. It opens on *Everyone*, so sharing as before is the same
  two presses.
- A version shared with a few is seen by **them, by whoever shared it, and by
  the administrators**. To everyone else it is not there: not in their Shared
  list, not at its name in a link, not to copy. Being told it exists and may
  not be opened would itself say something.
- **An administrator sees every one.** Somebody has to tidy up after a sharer
  who has gone, and the administrators already run the list of who sees
  anything at all. A publisher has no such sight, and sees what is shared with
  them.
- The Shared list says who each version is shared with, and **whoever shared
  it changes that** from the same place; so may an administrator. Someone it
  is shared with can open it and take a copy, and cannot share it further.
- **Shared again after a fix, a version keeps who it was shared with**, unless
  the new share says otherwise. A correction does not open it to everyone.
- **The published map is everyone's.** A version shared with a few cannot be
  published until it is shared with everyone, and the published one cannot be
  shared with fewer. Publish is held down on such a row and says why.
- Whether a version is restricted is **said outright**, not read off whether
  anyone is named for it. A version shared with one group does not fall open
  to everyone on the day that group is deleted: it is then its sharer's and
  the administrators'.

### Groups

- A group is **a name and the people in it**, and is the app's own: made by an
  administrator from people already on the list, with no directory and no
  security group behind it, and nobody's consent to ask for.
- **Groups** is the other view of Users & access, since a group is nothing but
  people from that list and is put together looking at it: name one, put
  people in from a list of who is not in it yet, take one out from their chip.
- Everyone who works on the map can read the groups — a version is shared with
  one by whoever shares it — and only an administrator changes them.
- One group to a name, whatever its case.

### Settings

- **A gear beside the avatar** opens what is set up or looked up rather than
  done to the map: **Theme**, then a button a row, each with a sentence saying
  what it is for — **Getting around**, **Users & access**, **Assistant
  model**, **Export**, **Export SVG**, **Import**.
- **A row is there for whoever may press it**, and not there at all for anyone
  else. A viewer's Settings is Theme, Getting around and the two exports. A
  button that is only waiting — Import, outside Edit mode — stays, greyed,
  with its sentence saying what it waits for.
- Pressing a row shuts Settings: what the press did is what should be in view.
- **The avatar opens what it says**: who you are, your role and what it may
  do, and Sign out.
- The header is left with what is done while reading or editing: Edit, the
  version, Draw, Teams, Slideshow, Assistant and About.

### The pips

- The row of dots along the foot of a shut capability, one a feature up to
  seven, is **half as large again and twice as dark**. It was a hint that had
  to be looked for, and a count is no use unless it is seen.
- On an oval that stands tall a full row is drawn a little smaller, since the
  oval is narrowest at its foot and the ends of the row would cross the rim.

### The file

Version 6. The teams are written at the foot of the file, after the lines,
each naming what it is on by key; after them the people, a list a layer. A
person's card that was moved is written where the bare key of its shape would
be, with where it was put:

```json
"teams": [
  { "key": "checkout", "title": "Checkout", "type": "Product team", "domains": ["billing"] },
  { "key": "platform", "title": "Platform", "type": "Enabling team", "areas": ["payments"], "capabilities": ["invoicing"] }
],
"productRoles": [
  { "key": "ada-example", "title": "Ada Example", "type": "Product lead", "icon": "female1.svg", "areas": ["payments"] }
],
"engineeringRoles": [
  {
    "key": "ben-sample", "title": "Ben Sample", "type": "Tech lead",
    "domains": ["billing", { "key": "lending", "x": 420, "y": -310 }]
  }
]
```

A card's place is measured from the shape it is on, in the map's units, and is
refused beyond 20,000 either way. A person who owns nothing in the file is not
read, and one who names a shape the file does not have is refused, with the
key that is missing.

An area, a domain or a capability says what its slide shows only when that is
not the shape alone, and names the picture uploaded for it:

```json
{ "key": "billing", "title": "Billing", "shape": { "position": "0,0", "postcard": "picture", "picture": "billing-screen.png" } }
```

`layers` lists five, and `types` may carry a `team`, a `productRole` and an
`engineeringRole` list beside the others. A figure is named as any icon is, by
its file's name. Which team is lit, which slide is open and whether the legend
is on the stage are none of them in the file: they are how the map is being
looked at.

### Under the hood

- Undo takes back what was typed. A title, a description or an owner is on
  the map as it is typed, so by the time the change was recorded the map
  already said the new thing, and Ctrl+Z put back the very words it was meant
  to take away. A field now keeps what it last handed over.
- Undo takes off an icon given to a shape that had none: the step handed back
  "no icon" in a form the map reads as nothing said.

## What it does not do

- **No link between a person on the map and a person on the list.** A name is
  typed; the list only offers. Nobody is removed from a map by being removed
  from Users & access.
- **No people on capabilities**, and no teams on features.
- **No moving a person from one layer to the other.** Someone added to the
  wrong one is added again on the right one.
- **Nothing drawn for a team**, and no cards for its members.
- **No slides for features**, touchpoints or actors, and no order of the
  deck's own: it is the map's.
- **No slides exported.** A deck is presented from the app.
- **No groups from a directory.** A group is made by hand from who has signed
  in or been added.
- **No sharing with a viewer.** A viewer sees the published map; one in a
  group is shown nothing by it.
- **Nothing for the Assistant.** The brief a model is shown names no teams and
  nobody who leads, so it cannot reason from either.

## Known issues

- A card the map hangs can still land on a neighbouring domain, a capability
  or a line: it is kept clear of other cards and of areas' names, and of
  nothing else. Dragging it is the answer.
- Nothing steps out of the way of a card that was put somewhere, another card
  included.
- A card picked up from a column is drawn as wide as its own words, so it
  narrows in the hand.
- The figures' faces are not filled in, so they are the colour of the card:
  light on a light map, dark on a dark one.
- The two layers' colours are told apart by hue. Beside each other they are
  warm and cool to nearly every eye; alone, a card says which layer it is on
  only to someone who has seen the other.
- A team lit from the legend dims the people's cards with the rest of the
  map, including the cards of whoever leads what the team is on.
- An uploaded picture that is replaced or no longer named by any slide stays
  in the store.
- Rolling back to 2.6.1 opens every shared version to every contributor, the
  restricted ones included.
- A new line cannot be let go on a connection point that another line already
  ends on: the line that is there lies over the point and takes the release,
  and the new line is dropped. The point either side of it takes it. Not new
  in this release; found while recording the demo for it.
