Getting around

Drag the map to pan, scroll to zoom, or use the controls at the bottom right. In Edit mode, click a connection point on a shape to start a connector. Press Edit in the header to make changes.

The layer control in the bottom left corner lists the three layers, topmost first: Presentation, Domains, and Areas underneath them both. The eye hides Presentation or Areas; Domains, the base layer, cannot be hidden, only dimmed. What you change there is for your tab alone — unless you are in Edit mode, where it sets what the map opens at for everyone.

In Edit mode each row also carries a box saying which layer you are adding to — one at a time. The Add buttons beside the control then offer that layer's own kinds — domains and capabilities, touchpoints and actors, or areas. Selecting a hidden layer shows it, and hiding the one you are working on moves you back to the base layer.

An area is who owns what: a band drawn round the domains, touchpoints and loose capabilities one team holds. It has no size of its own — it follows what it holds. Its title rides the border like a legend; Shift+Enter while renaming it breaks the title into rows, which stack outside the band. Drag a shape in from outside the line to put it in the area; to take it out, set Area to — in its details. Dragging a shape clear of its team only stretches the band after it, so tidying the map never reorganises the company. A shape is in one area or in none.

Assistant, in the header, takes the place of the details panel: pick what to ask, and either send it to the model this map is connected to or, where there is none, copy the prompt into the AI chat you use and paste its reply back. What comes back is a list of cards. Nothing on one touches the map until you apply it in Edit mode, and each card is one Ctrl+Z. Ask your own opens a box for your question, and Ctrl+Enter there sends it.

On macOS, use Cmd wherever this says Ctrl. While you type in a field, keys go to the field: Ctrl+Z there undoes typing, not a change to the map. Esc is the one key that also works from a field.

Palette

Edit palette, at the foot of the details panel, opens the palette editor in the bottom right corner, over a light overlay that leaves the map in view. Pick a swatch, then drag in the color area below the swatches or along the hue strip, or type a hex: the picker and the hex field follow each other as you go. A drag is applied when you let go; the hex field takes Enter to apply the color and Esc to put the old one back. Ctrl-Z undoes a color change, and Esc or Done closes the editor.

About Domain map

A shared map of your organization, so flow conversations start from context instead of a blank whiteboard.

The map is the fixed terrain: your domains and the business capabilities within them, each in a deliberate position. Whereas a diagram's layout is arbitrary — rearrange it and nothing is lost — a map anchors elements geometrically, so position carries meaning, and the terrain stays learnable across teams.

Flows are overlays. Trace a payment authorization from checkout through invoicing, financial booking, and settlement, and the route shows which teams are involved and how each participates in the end-to-end flow. Think of Google Maps as the terrain and your planned route as the overlay.

Establish the context once. Then draw on top of it — with a marker or digitally — every time after.
