Getting around

Drag the map to pan, scroll to zoom, or use the controls at the bottom right. Browsing, a drag pans from wherever it starts, on a shape or off one; in Edit mode a drag on a shape moves the shape, and open ground or the middle button pans. In Edit mode, click a connection point on a domain, a capability or a feature to start a connector, and a point on another to finish it. Press Edit in the header to make changes.

The map says more the closer you look, and nothing on it moves as you do. Zoom in on a capability and it opens onto its features — a row of pips along its foot says it holds some. Double-click a domain or a capability to close in on it, and Fit to screen to come back out.

The slider over the zoom buttons sets the level the lines are drawn at, without moving the map. F, where a map opens: every line where it really ends, on a feature, a capability or a domain. C: a line that ends on a feature leaves from its capability. D: every line leaves from a domain, the lines between two domains are one line, heavier the more it stands for, and each domain keeps its two strongest — point at one to read what it joins and how many it carries, and pick a domain to see every line it has. The level is the same in Edit mode, and is your tab's alone. A line drawn short of its own ends cannot be reshaped until the level, or the zoom, draws it whole.

The layer control in the bottom left corner lists the five layers, topmost first: Product roles and Engineering roles, which are who leads what — each marked with the colour its people's cards wear down their edge; then Presentation, Domains, and Areas underneath them all. The eye hides any of them but Domains, the base layer, which cannot be hidden, only dimmed; the half-disc beside it dims a layer instead. What you change there is for your tab alone — unless you are in Edit mode, where it sets what the map opens at for everyone.

In Edit mode each row also carries a box saying which layer you are adding to — one at a time. The Add buttons beside the control then offer that layer's own kinds — domains, capabilities and features, touchpoints and actors, areas, or a person in a role. A feature goes into the capability that is selected, after the ones it already holds, and a person is added on the area or the domain that is selected. Selecting a hidden layer shows it, and hiding the one you are working on moves you back to the base layer.

An area is who owns what: a band drawn round the domains, touchpoints and loose capabilities one team holds. It has no size of its own — it follows what it holds. Its title rides the border like a legend; Shift+Enter while renaming it breaks the title into rows, which stack outside the band. Drag a shape in from outside the line to put it in the area; to take it out, set Area to — in its details. Dragging a shape clear of its team only stretches the band after it, so tidying the map never reorganises the company. A shape is in one area or in none. An area says who answers for a part of the map today, and is there to be held against the domains, not to define them: a domain is cut by what the business does, whoever holds it.

Assistant, in the header, takes the place of the details panel: pick what to ask, and either send it to the model this map is connected to or, where there is none, copy the prompt into the AI chat you use and paste its reply back. What comes back is a list of cards. Nothing on one touches the map until you apply it in Edit mode, and each card is one Ctrl+Z. Ask your own opens a box for your question, and Ctrl+Enter there sends it.

Draw, in the header, puts a marker in your hand and locks the map under it: nothing on it is selected or moved while you draw, so a flow can be traced across the terrain without disturbing it. The tray in the top left corner holds five markers, an eraser, undo and clear. What you draw belongs to this tab — it is in no version, nobody else sees it, and it survives a refresh but not the tab being closed — and it goes when another version is opened in its place. Export SVG carries it into the picture. Draw stands down while Edit is on.

Product roles and Engineering roles are two layers of people: who leads an area or a domain for product, and who for engineering. Each person is a card — their icon, and beside it their name with their role under it — hung on what they own, and pressing one shows their details. In Edit mode, pick one of the two layers, select an area or a domain, and Add a product role or Add an engineering role; or name someone from the Product roles and Engineering roles lists in the shape's own details. A person has a name, a Role — type one in once, then pick it from the list — and an icon: one of the five figures the app carries, or anything from the icon library. Someone given a role takes the icon others in that role wear, until you pick another for them. A person may own several areas and domains, with a card on each, and one who owns nothing is taken off the map. A card starts by the name of what it is on: in Edit mode, drag it to wherever it reads better and it stays there, going with its shape when the shape is moved. Put cards back, in the person's details, hangs them by the names again.

Teams, in the header, opens a legend of the map's teams in the top right corner, under their Types. Press a team and the map is lit by it: what it is on is ringed, what it reaches through that — the capabilities of its domain, everything in its area — stays as drawn, and the rest stands back. Press a Type and every team of it is lit together. A lit team stays lit while you pick shapes; press it again, or put the legend away, and the map is the map again. In Edit mode the legend adds a team, and a team is put on an area, a domain or a capability from its own details or from the Teams list in the shape's.

Slideshow, in the header, is the map as a deck: a postcard for every area, every domain in it and each of the domain's capabilities, the shape as the map draws it beside its words. Across the top of a slide is where its shape sits — /area/domain/capability. Edit there makes the slide's words fields — they are the shape's own, so a change on a slide is a change to the map — and lets each slide choose its picture: the shape alone, the shape with its neighbours, or a picture of your own, uploaded for the slide. Present shows the slide in hand and nothing else, full screen where the browser allows it.

On macOS, use Cmd wherever this says Ctrl. While you type in a field, keys go to the field: Ctrl+Z there undoes typing, not a change to the map. Esc is the one key that also works from a field.

Palette

Edit palette, at the foot of the details panel, opens the palette editor in the bottom right corner, over a light overlay that leaves the map in view. Pick a swatch, then drag in the color area below the swatches or along the hue strip, or type a hex: the picker and the hex field follow each other as you go. A drag is applied when you let go; the hex field takes Enter to apply the color and Esc to put the old one back. Ctrl-Z undoes a color change, and Esc or Done closes the editor.

About Domain map

A shared map of your organization, so flow conversations start from context instead of a blank whiteboard.

The map is the fixed terrain: your domains and the business capabilities within them, each in a deliberate position. Whereas a diagram's layout is arbitrary — rearrange it and nothing is lost — a map anchors elements geometrically, so position carries meaning, and the terrain stays learnable across teams.

Flows are overlays. Trace a payment authorization from checkout through invoicing, financial booking, and settlement, and the route shows which teams are involved and how each participates in the end-to-end flow. Think of Google Maps as the terrain and your planned route as the overlay.

Establish the context once. Then draw on top of it — with a marker or digitally — every time after.
