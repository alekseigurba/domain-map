Getting around

Drag the map to pan, scroll to zoom, or use the controls at the bottom right. In Edit mode, click a connection point on a shape to start a connector. Press Edit in the header to make changes.

The layer control in the bottom left corner lists the map's layers, topmost first. The eye hides a layer above the base; the base layer cannot be hidden, only dimmed. What you change there is for your tab alone — unless you are in Edit mode, where it sets what the map opens at for everyone.

On macOS, use Cmd wherever this says Ctrl. While you type in a field, keys go to the field: Ctrl+Z there undoes typing, not a change to the map. Esc is the one key that also works from a field.

Palette

Edit palette, at the foot of the details panel, opens the palette editor in the bottom right corner, over a light overlay that leaves the map in view. Pick a swatch, then drag in the color area below the swatches or along the hue strip, or type a hex: the picker and the hex field follow each other as you go. A drag is applied when you let go; the hex field takes Enter to apply the color and Esc to put the old one back. Ctrl-Z undoes a color change, and Esc or Done closes the editor.

About Domain map

A shared map of your organization, so flow conversations start from context instead of a blank whiteboard.

The map is the fixed terrain: your domains and the business capabilities within them, each in a deliberate position. Whereas a diagram's layout is arbitrary — rearrange it and nothing is lost — a map anchors elements geometrically, so position carries meaning, and the terrain stays learnable across teams.

Flows are overlays. Trace a payment authorization from checkout through invoicing, financial booking, and settlement, and the route shows which teams are involved and how each participates in the end-to-end flow. Think of Google Maps as the terrain and your planned route as the overlay.

Establish the context once. Then draw on top of it — with a marker or digitally — every time after.
